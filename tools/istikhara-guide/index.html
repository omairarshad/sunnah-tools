Istikhara Prayer and Dua Guide

Learn how to pray two voluntary rakats for Istikhara and read the Arabic dua, transliteration and an original explanation with hadith references.

How to pray Salat al-Istikhara

Istikhara means seeking Allah’s guidance when deciding between permissible courses of action. It is not a method of predicting the future or replacing good judgement.

Four practical steps

Perform purification, pray two voluntary rak’ahs other than obligatory prayer, recite the reported supplication and mention the matter, then continue with thoughtful consultation and action.

What the supplication asks

The dua asks Allah to grant what is beneficial for religion, worldly life and ultimate outcome, to turn away harmful choices and to grant contentment with what is good.

Dreams and feelings

A dream, colour, special sensation or immediate sign is not a requirement in the authentic narration. If unsure, consult wise people, check relevant facts and keep making dua.

01When should you perform Istikhara?+

Istikhara is taught for matters where you are considering a permissible choice: for example, a suitable job, moving house or deciding between reasonable options. It is not a substitute for an obligation, a reason to pursue something prohibited or a replacement for advice on a dangerous situation.

Gather relevant facts, consult appropriately and ask Allah for guidance. The supplication asks that good be facilitated and harmful choices turned away.

Sahih al-Bukhari 1166 – Teaching of Istikhara↗

02Step-by-step method+

First perform the necessary purification. Pray two non-obligatory rakahs at a permissible time, then recite the reported dua and mention your specific matter. The precise position of the supplication relative to salam is discussed by scholars; follow credible local teaching.

There is no requirement to recite a specially assigned surah in each rakah. The reported dua is reproduced in Arabic in the interactive guide above.

  • Identify a permissible choice and gather facts
  • Make wudu as required and pray two voluntary rakahs
  • Recite the Istikhara dua with the matter in mind
  • Consult, decide and act responsibly; continue to make dua
03Understanding the supplication+

The words affirm that Allah knows what a person does not, has power over what a person cannot control, and can open or close a path according to what is truly good. The closing request is for the best outcome and contentment with it.

Arabic pronunciation and wording matter; the on-page Latin transliteration is approximate. For accuracy, learn the Arabic from a qualified teacher or trustworthy recorded recitation.

Arabic and English text – Bukhari 1166↗

04Do you need a dream, colour or feeling?+

The hadith itself teaches the two-rakah prayer and the dua; it does not say that a person must dream, see a colour or wait for a mystical sign. You should not insist that all obstacles or comforts are proof of one particular divine verdict.

After thoughtful effort, choose the most reasonable lawful course and trust in Allah while taking normal precautions.

05Repeating Istikhara and seeking counsel+

If the matter remains uncertain, you may continue making dua and seek further advice. The authenticity of particular fixed numbers of repetitions should be checked rather than invented.

Other people can offer good counsel, but a decision affecting your legal or medical wellbeing may require professional information as well as spiritual reflection.

06Worked example: deciding between two jobs+

You have two lawful job offers. Compare responsibilities, hours, pay, family impact and work conditions. Seek advice from people who understand your circumstances. Pray two rakahs and ask Allah to ease what is good.

If you later choose one role, this is not a claim that the site has predicted the unseen. It illustrates using prayer alongside responsible decision-making.

Further questions

Is Istikhara only for marriage?

No. It may be used for many permissible decisions.

Do I need to see a dream?

No. Dreams are not required by the authentic report.
