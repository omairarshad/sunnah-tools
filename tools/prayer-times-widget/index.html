How to embed a free prayer-time timetable on a website

  1. Search for your city or supply verified latitude and longitude.
  2. Check the IANA timezone such as Europe/London; daylight-saving changes follow that timezone.
  3. Match the Fajr/Isha method and Asr convention to your local mosque or qualified authority.
  4. Pick light or dark colours, a regular or compact display, and whether to include sunrise.
  5. Review the live preview, copy the iframe HTML and paste it into a page that supports custom HTML.
  6. Test mobile widths and check the times for at least one winter and summer day.

The embed is hosted by sunnah.tools. Your website needs permission to render external iframes, and visitors need an internet connection to load it. The embed page is intentionally marked noindex to avoid duplicating standalone tool pages.

What exactly do the five daily prayer times mean?

The widget lists Fajr, Dhuhr, Asr, Maghrib and Isha as calculated prayer start times. Sunrise is an astronomical reference time, not a sixth obligatory prayer. Congregation time (iqamah), the Adhan at a particular mosque, and prayer windows are not identical to a calculated start. Quran 4:103 refers to Salah at appointed times.

Why do Fajr and Isha times differ by calculation method?

Twilight-based calculation conventions use different solar depression angles. The Muslim World League, Karachi, ISNA North America, Umm al-Qura and other methods may therefore give noticeably different dawn or night times; high-latitude nights in the UK may require further local adjustment. For method comparisons and the impact of polar conditions use the Prayer Times Calculator and full calculation methods guide. The embed does not claim to know which convention your mosque uses.

How location and daylight saving affect embedded Salah times

The widget accepts a latitude, longitude and named timezone, not simply an arbitrary UTC offset. Named timezones incorporate seasonal clock changes; for example Europe/London uses different offsets in winter and summer. A visitor in another country still sees the selected mosque location's local clock. City names are descriptive labels, not geocoding proof: confirm coordinates match the label.

Worked example: embedding a London mosque prayer timetable

Suppose an organisation wants a compact widget for London. It chooses the example coordinates 51.5072° N, 0.1276° W, Europe/London, MWL method, standard Asr convention and a dark theme. The builder generates an iframe that always loads the selected location's calculated daily times, including daylight-saving adjustments.

These settings are an illustration, not a recommendation for a named London mosque. A mosque must verify its own method, seasonal announcements and published iqamah schedule before placing a widget prominently.

Iframe security, uptime and privacy considerations

Embeds load resources from sunnah.tools; the host site cannot guarantee they remain reachable if the service goes offline. The HTML does not collect worship history or visitor account data and does not require a sign-in. The default referrerpolicy reduces unnecessarily detailed cross-site referral information. Site administrators should review their own content security policy and external-embed consent requirements before installing third-party resources.

Troubleshooting blank frames and mobile sizing

If a frame is empty, verify its full HTTPS address, numerical coordinates and timezone. Some CMS editors remove iframes or block external frames by policy. Check the browser console and host platform embed permissions. Use the regular layout for a detailed card or compact layout where space is limited; do not force extremely small heights or crop the last prayer. Use the copyable widget URL to test the frame separately.

Widget versus a live mosque timetable

The widget does not retrieve an institution's verified Adhan and congregation times, prayer hall closures or local exceptions. Calculated prayer starts, actual moon-sighting events and a mosque's schedule are separate data products. For an authoritative congregation display, connect directly to the mosque's announcements instead of relabelling the calculated widget as official.

Frequently asked questions about prayer times widget

Can I embed prayer times on WordPress?

Yes, if your editor and hosting policy allow external iframe HTML. Test both desktop and mobile.

Does the widget cost money?

This builder and published widget are free to use; availability depends on the website remaining operational.

Does it show mosque iqamah times?

No; it shows estimated prayer starts based on a selected calculation convention.

Why are local mosque Fajr times different?

Prayer-time methods and high-latitude decisions differ. Match your local authority's schedule.

Does it update every day?

Yes. The embed recalculates for the configured timezone's current day while the page is open.

Does it work during daylight saving?

Its IANA timezone handles clock changes, subject to browser time-zone support.

Can I hide sunrise?

Yes. Sunrise may be shown as a reference or hidden; it is not counted as a sixth obligatory prayer.

Can I set Hanafi Asr?

Yes. The widget can use the Hanafi Asr convention or standard convention.

Does a visitor need to allow location access?

No. Your embedded location settings are explicitly entered in the builder.

Why is my iframe blank?

Check that your CMS permits iframes and the HTTPS address and settings are valid.

Can I use the embed on mobile?

Yes. The generated HTML is responsive within its container; test the selected height.

Does it use personal Salah records?

No. The embed displays time calculations and has no connection to the private Salah Tracker.

Common questions

Do embedded times match our mosque congregation times?+

Not necessarily. They are calculated prayer beginnings and may differ from local announced jamaat times. Compare methods and verify with your mosque before using.

Can I add the widget to WordPress?+

Usually yes. Paste the iframe into a Custom HTML block where your site permits embeds.

Will it show congregation times?+

No. It displays estimated prayer beginnings, not your mosque's scheduled congregation times.

Will a website widget show our mosque's iqamah schedule?+

Not unless that information is separately configured. A calculation-based widget cannot know congregation times set by the mosque.
