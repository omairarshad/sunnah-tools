Sunnah Fasting Planner

Plan voluntary Muslim fasting with Monday, Thursday, White Days, Ashura and Arafah dates, exclude Eid and Tashriq reminders, and download calendars.

Planning voluntary fasts through the year

The planner gathers Mondays, Thursdays, White Days and some significant recommended fasts. It is designed to plan optional observances while avoiding days on which ordinary voluntary fasting is prohibited or superseded by Ramadan.

Mondays, Thursdays and White Days

Reported practices include fasting on Mondays and Thursdays, and on the 13th, 14th and 15th Hijri dates. The planner may show overlapping motivations on a single date but exports only one calendar reminder per date.

Ashura and Arafah

Ashura falls on 10 Muharram; scholarly advice commonly includes an accompanying day. Arafah falls on 9 Dhul Hijjah, with specific considerations for pilgrims. These are included as references, not automatic personal obligations.

Excluded dates and uncertainty

The planner excludes Ramadan as a separate voluntary fast, Eid al-Fitr, Eid al-Adha and the days of Tashriq from optional calendar reminders. Calculated Umm al-Qura dates can differ from local moon-sighting announcements. Consult a clinician when fasting may affect your health.

01Mondays and Thursdays+

There are reports about the Prophet ﷺ fasting on Mondays and Thursdays. The planner lists these Gregorian weekdays as potential voluntary fasts and labels overlaps with lunar-date recommendations.

Whether a particular person should fast depends on health, obligations, ability and individual fiqh considerations.

Sunan Abi Dawud 2436 – Monday and Thursday↗

02White Days: 13, 14 and 15 Hijri+

Ayyam al-Bid are commonly associated with the 13th, 14th and 15th of each Islamic month. Our calculator converts dates using the Umm al-Qura scheme and allows an illustrative one-day adjustment, but it cannot know your local authority’s moon-sighting decision.

The 13th of Dhul Hijjah falls in the days of Tashriq and is not exported as an ordinary voluntary fast. Ramadan days are obligatory for those to whom fasting applies, not separate voluntary White Days.

Sunan Abi Dawud 2449 – White Days↗

03Ashura and the ninth of Muharram+

Ashura falls on 10 Muharram, with a report in Sahih Muslim that the Prophet ﷺ intended to also fast the ninth in a following year. The planner identifies these as date references; it does not claim a special fixed hour.

Some scholars discuss adding a day before or after Ashura. Confirm your preferred ruling and the local Islamic date.

Sahih Muslim 1134a – Ninth of Muharram↗

04The day of Arafah+

The ninth of Dhul Hijjah is the day of Arafah. Voluntary fasting on that day is generally discussed for people who are not performing Hajj; pilgrims have different practical and scholarly considerations.

The calendar labels the day “Arafah (for non-pilgrims)” rather than recommending it to everyone.

Sahih Muslim 1162b – Fasting Arafah↗

05Six fasts of Shawwal are flexible dates+

Sahih Muslim 1164a discusses six days of fasting in Shawwal after Ramadan. They are not six universal fixed dates on the calendar. The app therefore provides guidance on arranging them but does not automatically mark every Shawwal date as a required fast.

Whether missed Ramadan fasts must be made up before the six days, and sequencing advice, differ among scholars and depend on circumstances.

Sahih Muslim 1164a – Six days of Shawwal↗

06What does the calendar exclude?+

The two Eid days are explicitly prohibited fasting days in authentic hadith. The days of Tashriq (11–13 Dhul Hijjah) generally cannot be used for ordinary voluntary fasts; limited Hajj-related exceptions are discussed in fiqh.

Ramadan is never labelled as optional in this planner. You can show excluded dates in the table to see why they are not in calendar exports.

Sahih al-Bukhari 1990 – Two Eids↗

07Worked example and calendar export+

Suppose a Monday is also the 13th of a Hijri month. Both categories can appear on the same date; downloading the calendar creates one event, not two duplicate fasting obligations.

Use the category filters to focus on weekly dates, White Days or special dates, then export only eligible dates. Recheck with local moon sighting before acting.

Further questions

Does it include the six days of Shawwal automatically?

No. Those six dates can be chosen flexibly, so the guide explains them rather than presenting arbitrary fixed Shawwal dates.

Why do some dates disappear from the export?

Ramadan, Eid and ordinary voluntary fasting during Tashriq are excluded. User-selected category filters may also reduce the export.

Can I import the calendar as guaranteed local dates?

No. Every Hijri conversion is an estimate that must be checked against local announcements.

Educational guidance only. Where dates, local rules, clinical decisions or legal outcomes matter, verify them with a qualified professional or religious authority. No live moon-sighting or medical decisions are provided.
