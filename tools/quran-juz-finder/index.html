What is a Juz in the Quran?

A Juz is one of 30 conventional reading sections of the Quran. The singular is Juz; plural Ajza. In South Asian usage, a part is often called a Para. Dividing recitation into thirty portions can support structured reading, especially during Ramadan, but a Juz is not necessarily a thematic unit or a new Surah. The Quran has 114 Surahs; a Juz may begin part-way through one.

Where do the 30 Juz begin and end?

Each entry lists the first Surah and Ayah of a Juz, plus the first reference of the next Juz. That next boundary is exclusive: for example, Juz 1 begins at 1:1 and continues until just before 2:142, where Juz 2 begins. Juz 30 starts at 78:1 and runs through the last verse of Surah An-Nas, 114:6. The table avoids inventing an exact final verse where another Surah ends and does not translate verse boundaries into page counts.

A Juz number and Surah number are different references: Juz 30 starts at Surah 78, not Surah 30.

Are Juz always twenty pages long?

In the familiar 604-page Madani Mushaf, many Juz correspond roughly to twenty pages, but the opening pages and edition-specific pagination mean that an unqualified 'twenty pages per Juz' rule should not be used to locate a boundary. Indo-Pak scripts and other printed editions can use different page layouts. Quran Planner uses user-specified page counts; this finder uses Surah and Ayah references.

Worked example: finding Juz 30 (Amma)

Search for Juz 30. The selected part begins at Surah An-Naba, 78:1 and finishes at Surah An-Nas, 114:6. It includes many short Surahs often studied for Salah and Hifz. Open the external link to begin at Quran 78:1.

Searching for 2:142 instead locates the beginning of Juz 2 in Surah Al-Baqarah. These references describe boundaries, not the number of pages a particular Mushaf devotes to them.

How to divide Quran reading across Ramadan

One Juz per day creates a thirty-part reading goal. If the month has 29 days, a fixed thirty-day schedule needs an adjustment. Readers may divide a Juz into Hizb subdivisions, sessions, or page ranges depending on their edition. Reading attentively and consistently matters more than promising an unsupported obligatory pace. See Quran 73:4 on measured recitation and the reading-plan calculator.

Juz, Hizb, Rub al-Hizb and Manzil explained

A Juz is one of thirty major recitation parts. A Hizb commonly denotes half a Juz, while smaller markers such as Rub al-Hizb help portion reading into shorter sessions. Other regional systems such as Manzil may divide the Quran differently. These navigation markers are reading aids rather than new revealed chapter boundaries.

Accuracy of verse boundaries, translations and recitation

The list follows a conventional thirty-Juz verse-start index. It is a reference directory, not a substitute for a reliable Quran text or teacher. Check any specialised edition or alternative reading division against a trusted Mushaf. The page does not reproduce a translation, and no automatic Tajwid ruling is inferred from an index.
