Daily Adhkar and Quranic Duas: a complete sourced remembrance guide

Short answer: Adhkar (singular dhikr) is remembrance of Allah. A useful daily routine may include Quranic supplications and distinct morning, evening and after-Salah Sunnah reports. They should not be merged into one invented repetition schedule. This tool links each Quranic dua to its verse and each hadith-based remembrance to its report.

What are Adhkar and Duas?

Dhikr refers to remembering Allah and dua to supplication. They overlap but are not identical labels. Quranic verses can contain supplications, while narrated Sunnah may describe specific words, settings, numbers and benefits. Quran 13:28 connects remembrance of Allah with the tranquillity of hearts. Our app clearly separates text taken from Quranic verses from cards that point to authentic hadith reports.

Five Quranic supplications with sources

The collection includes the plea for knowledge (20:114), good in this world and the next (2:201), steadfast hearts (3:8), the supplication of Yunus (21:87), and mercy for parents (17:24). The on-page Arabic and short meaning are excerpts and explanations; click each source to inspect the Quranic context and translation.

Morning and evening Sunnah Adhkar

Reported daily remembrance can include reading Al-Ikhlas, Al-Falaq and An-Nas at morning and evening; see Sunan Abi Dawud 5082. The description, exact count and grading should be read in context rather than assigned indiscriminately to every Quranic dua. Sahih al-Bukhari 6306 also reports the well-known Sayyid al-Istighfar supplication with timing and virtue. The tool links to these complete texts instead of supplying a partial unsourced Arabic rendition.

Dhikr after Salah and the 33/33 patterns

Among the transmitted practices is the after-prayer remembrance of SubhanAllah, Alhamdulillah and Allahu Akbar in reported counts. Sahih Muslim 597a describes 33 repetitions of each followed by a final Tawhid declaration. Other authentic reports provide alternate patterns. The Tasbih Counter offers separate selectable variants rather than pretending they are all the same.

How to build a manageable daily remembrance habit

  1. Choose an established authentic source appropriate to the time or occasion.
  2. Read its Arabic and understand the meaning as well as you can.
  3. Follow any reported count only when the particular narration supports it.
  4. Keep the core routine manageable and consistent, without burdening yourself with dozens of unverified claims.
  5. Use the optional reading tally only as a personal aid, not proof of religious merit.
  6. For Arabic pronunciation and wording seek a reliable teacher or recitation source.

Worked example: a morning and after-Salah remembrance routine

A reader chooses Quran 20:114 for an unscheduled personal supplication; the app does not set a mandatory repetition count for it. Separately, after an obligatory Salah, the reader may follow the reported 33 SubhanAllah, 33 Alhamdulillah and 33 Allahu Akbar plus final declaration from Sahih Muslim 597a.

These are different categories: the first is a Quranic dua available for personal recitation, while the second is an occasion-specific narrated format. Avoid describing either as a full replacement for the complete daily Sunnah.

How to recognise authentic Adhkar and avoid fabricated rewards

Verify exact wording, Arabic vowels, the hadith reference and its assessment, the context (morning, evening, after prayer, travel or sleep), and whether a stated number is actually transmitted. A social-media image that promises a precise worldly reward without evidence is not sufficient. Quran.com and numbered hadith collections let readers trace a statement rather than trusting a paraphrase.

Reading controls, Arabic text and privacy

The Quranic dua reading counter is intentionally temporary: changing entries resets it, and it does not create an account or store a sensitive worship history. The app provides brief English meanings, which are not substitutes for a comprehensive tafsir. Follow the linked verses for the full wording and meaning.

Frequently asked questions about daily adhkar

What is daily Adhkar?

Voluntary remembrance of Allah, including occasion-specific narrated practices.

What is the difference between dhikr and dua?

Dhikr means remembrance; dua is supplication, and they can overlap.

Are Quranic duas automatically repeated 33 times?

No. A hadith-based count for one remembrance should not be assigned to every Quranic supplication.

What is Sayyid al-Istighfar?

An established supplication for forgiveness; see Sahih al-Bukhari 6306 for its wording.

Which short Surahs are recited in morning and evening dhikr?

Al-Ikhlas, Al-Falaq and An-Nas are mentioned in a report at Abu Dawud 5082.

Can I use the collection without Arabic transliteration?

Yes. Arabic and English meanings are shown, with direct Quran verse links.

Is this a complete morning/evening collection?

No. It is a curated set of Quranic duas and source pointers.

Can I count my readings?

Yes. The personal count does not prescribe a particular number.

Do the references link to primary sources?

Each Quranic dua links to Quran.com and the Sunnah examples to numbered hadith pages.

Is the English meaning a full tafsir?

No. Read a reliable translation and commentary for context.

Can I save a history of my dhikr here?

What about scholarly differences?

Check the report, its grading and your religious teacher when making a regular prescribed practice.
