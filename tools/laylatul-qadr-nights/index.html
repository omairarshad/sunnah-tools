Laylatul Qadr: the last ten nights, odd nights, dua and complete guide

Short answer: Seek Laylatul Qadr throughout Ramadan's last ten nights, paying special attention to odd-numbered nights. The commonly listed 21st, 23rd, 25th, 27th and 29th nights are planning dates, not predictions that any single evening is definitely Laylatul Qadr.

What does the Quran say about Laylatul Qadr?

Surah al-Qadr (Quran 97:1–5) associates the night with the sending down of the Quran, describes its immense virtue and refers to peace until dawn. Quran 97:3 places its value beyond a thousand months. The text does not identify a fixed Gregorian date that an app can reveal.

The name is often transliterated Laylat al-Qadr or Laylatul Qadr; it is also called the Night of Decree or Night of Power in English. The calculator answers which civil evenings correspond to numbered Ramadan nights, not which one is certainly the blessed night.

Which are the odd nights of the last ten days of Ramadan?

Sahih al-Bukhari 2017 directs seeking Laylatul Qadr in odd nights of the last ten. Common planning calendars highlight the 21st, 23rd, 25th, 27th and 29th Islamic nights, counted from the beginning of Ramadan. They are invitations to increase devotion, not five exclusive guaranteed dates.

Why not focus only on the 27th? It has an important place in some reports and communal customs, but no calculator, observation of moon phase or fixed civil-date algorithm establishes that it must be Laylatul Qadr every year. A sustained approach during all the last ten nights is more faithful to the broader guidance.

How do the last ten nights differ in a 29-day versus a 30-day Ramadan?

If Ramadan lasts 30 nights

The final ten are nights 21–30. Odd numbers from the start are 21, 23, 25, 27 and 29.

If Ramadan lasts 29 nights

The final ten are nights 20–29. The same five odd-numbered nights occur within them, but the ten-night interval begins one night earlier.

Sahih al-Bukhari 2021 uses wording that counts nights remaining until the end of Ramadan. The parity of a night counted backward can differ from its number counted forward when month length is different. This is a further reason to seek Laylatul Qadr throughout the closing nights instead of restricting worship to a single list.

The final length of Ramadan is not independently known to this tool and may be confirmed only with a relevant Shawwal announcement. The 29/30 selector changes planning boundaries, not religious facts.

When do the 21st and 27th nights begin?

Islamic nights begin at sunset on the preceding civil evening. The 21st night starts at local Maghrib after the twentieth daytime fast; the 27th night starts after the twenty-sixth daytime fast. A Gregorian calendar event for an evening should be labelled with that evening's civil date, not the following morning's date.

The planner deliberately outputs calendar-day markers, not precise Maghrib alarms. It cannot confirm the user's horizon, prayer method or locality from a date field. Use the Suhoor & Iftar Times or your mosque's schedule to check the actual sunset.

Authentic Laylatul Qadr dua: Arabic, transliteration and meaning

When Aisha asked the Prophet ﷺ what to say if she recognised the Night of Decree, a supplication asking Allah's pardon was narrated in Jami at-Tirmidhi 3513 (displayed as Sahih in Sunnah.com's grading). Wording variants include an additional description of Allah's generosity; check the linked report for context.

اللَّهُمَّ إِنَّكَ عَفُوٌّ تُحِبُّ الْعَفْوَ فَاعْفُ عَنِّي

Transliteration: Allahumma innaka ʿafuwwun tuhibbu al-ʿafwa faʿfu ʿanni.

Meaning: O Allah, You are Pardoning and love pardon, so pardon me.

Source: Jami at-Tirmidhi 3513. Presented as a reported supplication, not a condition for worship or a required repetition count.

Personal duas can also be made sincerely in one's own language. This page does not invent a fixed number of recitations or imply an Arabic formula is mandatory for receiving the night's blessings.

How to plan the last ten nights sustainably

  1. Protect the core obligations. Keep the daily prayers and responsibilities in view while making a realistic nightly plan.
  2. Make room for night prayer and dua. Use time for sincere supplication, repentance, reflection and voluntary worship according to your ability.
  3. Read the Quran with care. Small, consistent portions can be more sustainable than an unrealistic target; the Quran Planner is an optional organisational aid.
  4. Remember family and community. Support others and give charity according to means; the calculator does not prescribe a special amount.
  5. Keep all ten evenings visible. Use the full view, particularly while the month length remains provisional.
  6. Confirm local dates. An imported .ics file is static and may need replacement if the first fasting day was announced differently.

If the first daytime fast were 8 February 2027, the 21st night would begin at sunset on 27 February 2027; the 27th night at sunset on 5 March 2027; and the 29th night at sunset on 7 March 2027.

A 30-night plan displays nights 21–30; a 29-night plan displays nights 20–29. These calendar dates illustrate the numbering arithmetic and are not an official Ramadan or Laylatul Qadr announcement for 2027.

All-day calendar markers are deliberately not labelled as exact prayer times. They help people reserve an evening for worship; they do not trigger a Maghrib-time notification.

Laylatul Qadr frequently asked questions

What is Laylatul Qadr?

The Night of Decree is described in Surah al-Qadr (Quran 97) as a night of extraordinary blessing associated with the Quran’s revelation. Its precise occurrence is not provided by a calendar.

Is Laylatul Qadr always on Ramadan 27?

No. The 27th is often emphasised in some communities, but Sahih al-Bukhari 2017 calls for seeking it in the odd nights of the last ten; it is not guaranteed on one numbered night.

Which five odd-numbered nights should I plan?

When counting from the beginning of Ramadan, the commonly highlighted nights are 21, 23, 25, 27 and 29. Plan worship throughout all the final ten nights, not only these five.

What changes if Ramadan is 29 nights long?

If the month has 29 nights, the final ten are numbered 20 through 29; in a 30-night month they are 21 through 30. This is why the tool shows both month-length scenarios.

Why might odd nights be discussed as “nights remaining”?

Some reports count backward from the end of the month (see Sahih al-Bukhari 2021). When the eventual month length differs, an odd count from the end may not match an odd number from the start.

When does Ramadan night 21 start?

At local sunset after the twentieth daytime fast, before the Gregorian daytime date of Ramadan 21. Enter the first fasting date to calculate the relevant civil evening.

What time does the Night of Decree begin?

The Islamic night begins at local sunset and runs into the following pre-dawn period. This tool displays evening calendar dates, not an observed or calculated sunset clock time.

Can I know for certain which night it was?

No. A calendar can number evenings, but it cannot reveal or certify the actual Night of Decree. The uncertainty encourages worship across the final ten.

Which dua is reported for Laylatul Qadr?

Jami at-Tirmidhi 3513 reports a supplication asking Allah for pardon. The page provides an Arabic wording, transliteration, meaning and source, without treating it as a compulsory formula.

Will the downloaded calendar notify me exactly at Maghrib?

No. The ICS file contains all-day markers on the relevant civil evenings. Check your local Maghrib for an actual sunset time and update the calendar if your Ramadan dates change.
