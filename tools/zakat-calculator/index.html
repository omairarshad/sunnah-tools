How to calculate Zakat on savings, gold and other wealth

Zakat al-Mal is an obligation associated with qualifying wealth. A useful calculation begins by understanding the minimum threshold (Nisab), the relevant lunar year (hawl) and which assets and liabilities belong in the assessment. The calculator above handles the arithmetic; this guide explains the decisions you must make before trusting an estimate.

How to calculate Zakat, step by step

  1. Choose the assessment date

    Identify the date used to review your wealth and check whether a lunar year (hawl) is relevant. If you are uncertain when your Zakat year began, get qualified guidance rather than assuming that the previous Ramadan is your personal anniversary.

  2. List qualifying assets

    Add cash, savings, gold and silver, relevant trade stock and investment values. Enter only the part you have determined is zakatable. Avoid counting one asset in two categories.

  3. Apply the appropriate liabilities

    Review payments you actually owe and which liabilities are deductible in your circumstances. Be careful with the entire balance of long-term loans and ongoing living costs.

  4. Check the Nisab benchmark

    Enter verified gold and silver prices for the same day and currency. Select the gold or silver reference according to guidance you follow; either benchmark can produce a different eligibility threshold.

  5. Calculate the conditional amount

    Subtract qualifying liabilities from qualifying assets, compare the result with the selected Nisab, and calculate 2.5% of net qualifying wealth where the conditions are met.

  6. Verify before paying

    Confirm hawl, asset treatments and eligibility with trusted religious guidance, then direct Zakat to eligible recipients or a suitable charitable organisation.

The general arithmetic used for a qualifying wealth assessment is:

Qualifying assets − permitted liabilities = net qualifying wealth If the applicable Nisab and lunar-year conditions are met: Net qualifying wealth × 0.025 = conditional Zakat estimate

As with the established asset-category approach in the Islamic Relief UK Zakat calculator, the point is to avoid leaving out relevant holdings or deducting every debt indiscriminately.

What are Nisab and hawl?

Nisab — the minimum wealth threshold

The Nisab is a benchmark derived from traditional gold or silver weights. Common UK reference amounts are 87.48 grams of gold and 612.36 grams of silver. Their equivalent monetary amounts vary with metal prices. The silver benchmark is often lower; differences between gold- and silver-based assessments can be significant. See Islamic Relief UK's Nisab discussion.

For example, if a verified price is 80 currency units per gram of 24-carat gold, the gold Nisab is 6,998.40 units. If silver is 1.20 units per gram, the silver Nisab is approximately 734.83 units. These are invented illustration prices, not market quotes or the Nisab for today. Use your own current prices in the calculator.

Hawl — the relevant lunar year

A hawl is an Islamic lunar year, approximately 354 or 355 days. It is not automatically the same as 365 Gregorian days. The starting date of the Zakat year and the treatment of wealth that rises or falls during it can depend on the legal school and the type of property. The tool therefore asks you to acknowledge that you have reviewed the applicable requirement rather than deciding it for you.

See Islamic Relief's explanation of hawl and our Zakat anniversary reminder. A reminder date is not a verdict that Zakat has become due.

Gold or silver — which should I select?

Different scholarly schools and organisations apply or recommend different references. The calculator lets you select either, and shows both thresholds when both prices are provided. It does not automatically choose the lower threshold or declare which view is binding in your circumstances.

Gold jewellery and purity

Do not multiply the gross weight of 18-carat or 22-carat jewellery by a pure 24-carat price and assume that is its qualifying value. Purity affects the amount of fine gold. The calculator offers either weight-based entry or the option to enter an independently assessed monetary value. Whether personal jewellery is included remains a fiqh question that varies between schools.

For a detailed gold-only estimate, use the Gold Zakat Calculator; for financial investments, the Stocks and Crypto Zakat estimator explains some additional limitations.

Debts, pensions, stocks and common complications

Which debts can be deducted?

Do not assume that the full outstanding mortgage, all future rent payments, personal credit limits or ordinary household expenses can be subtracted. Debt deductibility varies, and often concerns amounts actually due rather than a lifetime of future obligations. Enter only the permitted portion identified by your trusted guidance. A very large debt entry may reduce the arithmetic estimate to zero, but that does not settle which debts should legally have been deducted.

What about pensions and shares?

Whether and how a pension is zakatable depends on the pension's structure, ownership and access. Shares held for trading may be treated differently from long-term holdings, and financial funds can contain varied underlying assets. The tool provides an eligible investment value field, but does not infer that value from an entire pension pot or share price. Get qualified advice on the relevant portion before entering it.

Does rent or salary itself attract Zakat?

The ordinary cash-wealth calculation is about qualifying wealth in your possession, not simply multiplying every salary payment or monthly rental receipt by 2.5%. Rental property held for income, the rent you retain, and property held for sale can be treated differently. Use guidance specific to the asset's ownership and purpose.

When is Zakat paid, and who can receive it?

Zakat al-Mal does not have one universal Ramadan due date; it is connected to the person's applicable lunar-year assessment. Quran 9:60 outlines categories of eligible recipients. If giving through a charity, check that it distributes Zakat according to your requirements and that the donation is designated as Zakat, not another type of charity.

Zakat al-Mal is not Zakat al-Fitr (Fitrana). The latter is a separate Ramadan-related obligation with its own rules and per-person calculation. See the Fitrana Calculator or the Nisab Calculator for focused help.

Zakat questions people commonly ask

Is Zakat 2.5% of all the money I've earned this year?

No. The 2.5% figure is applied to qualifying net wealth under the relevant conditions, not necessarily the full sum of salaries received during the year. Consider what you owned at your assessment date and how your school treats the holdings.

Do I have to pay Zakat on my home and car?

A home and vehicle for ordinary personal use are generally distinguished from property held as merchandise. Property acquired to trade or sell may be treated differently, so review the specific intention and ownership.

Can I use the silver Nisab even if the gold threshold is higher?

The gold and silver thresholds are both shown because schools and advisers use different approaches. The lower threshold can increase the number of people assessed as eligible. Follow an approach endorsed by qualified guidance appropriate to you.

Should I count gold jewellery?

That depends on its use, purity and scholarly school. Do not assume personal jewellery is either universally included or excluded. If included, use an appropriate qualifying valuation rather than a gross-weight calculation using an unsuitable purity price.

Can I deduct the whole mortgage balance?

Not automatically. The deductibility of immediate versus long-term debt and future payments differs. Enter only the amount you have reasonably determined qualifies under your chosen ruling.

Does Zakat have to be paid during Ramadan?

No universal Ramadan date applies to everyone. For qualifying forms of wealth, the relevant date follows your lunar-year (hawl) circumstances; some people choose Ramadan for voluntary giving, but that does not by itself reset an existing obligation.

Does the calculator save my financial details?

No financial inputs are stored by this calculator. The figures remain in the page while it is open. You can explicitly download a text summary, which is saved wherever your browser handles downloads; take care with sensitive financial files.

Common questions

Is this a final ruling on the Zakat I owe?+

No. It is an illustrative planning estimate. Confirm asset treatment, debt deductions, hawl and the appropriate Nisab with qualified religious guidance.

Is Zakat always 2.5%?+

The familiar 2.5% rate applies to many qualifying forms of wealth, but not all assets or obligations follow the same rule.

Which Nisab should I use?+

Gold and silver give different thresholds. Apply the approach recommended by a trusted scholar or your relevant institution.

Should I enter every pension balance as cash?+

Not necessarily. Pension ownership, access and the guidance you follow can affect its Zakat treatment. Review those factors before entering a qualifying amount.

How do I calculate Zakat on my savings?+

Include qualifying cash and bank balances, then assess other relevant assets and permitted debts. Compare net wealth with the applicable gold- or silver-based Nisab; where the lunar-year and other conditions apply, 2.5% is the common rate for qualifying money.

Is Zakat due on the full mortgage balance?+

Do not automatically deduct the entire balance of a long-term mortgage. Debt deductibility can depend on when amounts are due and the legal school followed; only enter liabilities allowed by qualified guidance.

How do I calculate Zakat on gold jewellery?+

First determine whether the jewellery is included under the guidance you follow. When it is included, use a reliable qualifying value or the correct fine-gold content and appropriate price; do not value lower-carat jewellery as if all its weight were pure 24-carat gold.

Do stocks and pensions count towards Zakat?+

Their treatment depends on factors such as ownership, access, investment purpose and underlying assets. The tool asks for the qualifying value as determined under the guidance you use, rather than assuming an entire pension balance or portfolio market value is automatically zakatable.

Is Zakat automatically due every Ramadan?+

No. Zakat al-Mal is tied to the relevant lunar-year (hawl) conditions for the individual. Ramadan is not a universal anniversary date. If you are unsure, seek guidance on the start and continuity of your Zakat year.

Can I download my Zakat calculation without saving it online?+

Yes. The summary is generated on your device when you choose to download it. The calculator does not use browser storage for the financial amounts entered; protect any downloaded file as you would another personal financial document.
