When does Ramadan begin and end?

Ramadan is the ninth month of the Islamic lunar calendar. Quran 2:185 identifies Ramadan as the month of the Quran and sets out the obligation of fasting, with allowances and provisions discussed in the verse. Sahih al-Bukhari 1909 connects starting and ending the month’s fast with the crescent and gives guidance when visibility is obstructed.

In a Hijri calendar, the religious date changes at sunset. If a community announces that Ramadan begins on the evening before a Monday, Monday is the first civil fasting day. This tool asks for the first Gregorian fasting day, not the evening of the crescent announcement.

The calendar’s initial suggestion uses an algorithmic Umm al-Qura date. It is explicitly labelled as an estimate, since communities may follow local sightings, other regional sighting policies or a different computed calendar. Neither the calculator nor the algorithm can independently confirm a sighting.

Before printing: Check your local authority’s first-day announcement. Near month-end, confirm whether the final fast is day 29 or day 30 and when Shawwal/Eid al-Fitr begins. A pre-generated month is not a final religious calendar.

How to create a reliable 29- or 30-day timetable

  1. Choose the location where you will fast

    Search the city directory, select a preset, or enter both coordinates. Confirm that the IANA timezone belongs to that location. Travellers should create a different timetable for their destination.

  2. Enter the first Gregorian fasting day

    The suggested date comes from a calculated Umm al-Qura calendar. Replace it with the date announced by the recognised authority you follow; the first fast is on the civil day after the preceding sunset.

  3. Choose 29 or 30 days

    For early planning, 30 days shows the longest possible month. A lunar month may end after 29 days; update the table rather than assuming every planned day is Ramadan.

  4. Match your local Fajr calculation method

    Use the same method and high-latitude convention as your mosque where possible. If the calculated result disagrees with an authoritative local timetable, check the settings and seek clarification before relying on it.

  5. Read Fajr and Maghrib as daily boundaries

    Fajr marks the calculated start of the daily fast; Maghrib is the calculated sunset end. An optional earlier reminder is only a planning aid and never changes the Fajr result.

  6. Review the full month before exporting

    Look for unavailable rows, changes across weeks and daylight saving. Print for the fridge, download a CSV for a spreadsheet, or import calendar events only after confirming both dates and local prayer-time practice.

The table displays the Ramadan day number, Gregorian weekday/date, calculated Fajr, calculated Maghrib, and approximate fast duration. An optional early preparation reminder can be shown in a separate column; it is not the fast’s start time.

Suhoor, imsak, Fajr and Iftar: which times matter?

Quran 2:187 gives the daily fasting boundaries: eating and drinking until dawn becomes clear, then completing the fast until night. In the timetable, Fajr estimates the true-dawn start and Maghrib estimates sunset, when iftar begins.

Suhoor is the pre-dawn meal, encouraged in Sahih al-Bukhari 1923. Some calendars show an earlier imsak as a precaution or planning signal. There is no universal additional ten-minute religious cutoff established by the timetable itself. When in doubt about your mosque’s dawn assessment, use qualified local guidance.

Why do Ramadan schedules differ between mosques and cities?

Location and horizon

Dawn and sunset depend on latitude, longitude, the date, elevation and local horizon. The tool calculates astronomical estimates, not observations from a particular minaret or hill.

Fajr angles and methods

Organisations adopt different twilight angles or local conventions. The calculator offers several established software presets and a first-day comparison, but it cannot decide which one a specific mosque uses.

High latitudes and polar conditions

During northern summer, astronomical twilight can behave unusually or fail to reach a selected angle. The available approximation rules are selectable and disclosed, never treated as a binding religious verdict.

Daylight saving time

The displayed clocks use the named place’s IANA timezone on each day. For example, Europe/London normally switches between GMT and BST; do not manually add another hour to a displayed BST time.

The underlying implementation uses the open-source Adhan JavaScript astronomical library. Its outputs are UTC instants; the tool converts them to local clock times for the selected timezone. If an instant is implausible for that civil day, the corresponding row is marked Unavailable, not silently substituted with sunrise or another city’s time.

Especially in northern England, Scotland and polar regions, a software setting may not match the practice followed by local religious authorities. Resolve discrepancies before relying on the timetable for the actual fast.

Printing, CSV and calendar events — what each export contains

  • Print / Save PDF: uses the browser’s print dialog to produce a clean, full-month timetable with location, timezone, method and provisional-date warnings. Choose PDF in the print dialog to save a file.
  • CSV: includes all days, the calculated times, approximate duration and any optional reminder, along with location, timezone, method, high-latitude and polar settings. Unavailable cells are explicitly marked; user-supplied labels are protected from spreadsheet formula execution.
  • ICS calendar: includes Fajr and Maghrib events as true UTC instants, with calculation context. It is disabled if any day lacks a valid pair of times. A calendar import will not automatically refresh after dates or settings change.

Ramadan timetable FAQs

When does Ramadan actually begin?

The Islamic month begins at sunset, and its first day of fasting follows at dawn. The calendar start depends on the crescent/calendar policy of the local authority you follow; a generated Gregorian date is not a sighting announcement.

Why can neighbouring countries begin Ramadan on different days?

Authorities may use local or wider moon sightings, testimony requirements or distinct calendar policies. The tool lets you enter the start date but does not adjudicate between those practices.

Is Ramadan 29 or 30 days?

Both lengths occur. The provisional 30-day grid is useful for planning, but the final fasting day and Eid al-Fitr depend on the relevant Shawwal announcement.

Do I stop eating at imsak or Fajr?

The primary Quranic daily boundary is true dawn, represented here by calculated Fajr. An optional reminder a few minutes earlier can help preparation but is not a separate universal obligation. Verify Fajr with your mosque.

Is Iftar at Maghrib or the Isha prayer?

Iftar is associated with sunset, the beginning of Maghrib, not Isha. The tool displays calculated Maghrib for each day. Confirm the local horizon and timetable where relevant.

Do the table times equal my mosque’s adhan or iqamah?

No. These are astronomical estimates using a chosen method. A mosque may use different dawn calculations, local adjustments or congregation timings.

What if Fajr is unavailable at high latitudes?

The tool retains the row and marks it unavailable rather than replacing dawn with sunrise. Polar and twilight rules differ; seek the applicable local authority’s guidance, particularly for northern summer.

Does the timetable account for BST and other clock changes?

Yes, displayed times are formatted in the selected IANA timezone for each date. Calendar events use UTC instants so imported events can follow timezone changes, provided the place and timezone were entered correctly.

Can I use the calendar while travelling?

Only for the location it was generated for. Recalculate for the destination, check the timezone and any changes in local dawn or sunset. Travel-related fasting exemptions are separate jurisprudential matters.

Will the download update automatically after a moon-sighting announcement?

No. Printed, CSV and imported .ics files are static copies. Change the dates and regenerate them if the local announcement or calculation settings change.

Does the tool calculate Taraweeh or congregational prayer times?

No. It shows the daily Fajr and Maghrib estimates for fasting. The timing and arrangement of Taraweeh and mosque congregation are matters for the local mosque timetable.

Is my location or Ramadan timetable uploaded?

The calculation runs in your browser. Shared city/coordinate and prayer-method preferences may be stored locally in that browser; downloads happen when you choose them. The tool does not maintain an account or upload your timetable to a prayer-time service.

Common questions

Does the first date or the calendar establish Ramadan?+

No. The initial Umm al-Qura suggestion is algorithmic, not an observed crescent or official announcement. Confirm the first and last day of fasting with your recognised local authority.

Does the timetable tell me when Ramadan officially starts?+

No. You can edit the algorithmic suggested date to match your recognised local moon-sighting or calendar announcement.

What is the difference between Fajr and imsak?+

Fajr estimates true dawn and the start of fasting. An optional earlier reminder may help with meal preparation but does not establish another universal religious cutoff.

Can Ramadan be 29 rather than 30 days?+

Yes. Select either length, then update it after your local Shawwal announcement confirms the end of the month.

Does the printable calendar show daylight-saving time changes?+

Yes. Each day's actual calculated UTC instants are displayed in the selected place's timezone, including relevant clock changes.

Can I save a Ramadan timetable as PDF?+

Use Print / Save PDF and choose Save as PDF in your browser's print dialog. The print layout contains the month and its settings.

What is included in the Ramadan CSV?+

All dates, Fajr, Maghrib, estimated fasting duration, optional reminder and the calculation location, time zone and method are included. Unavailable times are identified.

Can I download an ICS Ramadan calendar?+

Yes, when all daily times are valid. Calendar events contain actual UTC instants, but imported events do not update when a sighting announcement or setting changes.

Why does my mosque's Fajr differ from the table?+

It may use a different twilight angle, local observation, high-latitude convention or adjustment. Select the matching convention and consult your mosque for authoritative times.

How does this work at high latitudes?+

You can select an astronomical fallback, but impossible or inconsistent results are labelled unavailable. Follow local scholarly guidance for polar or persistent-twilight situations.

Is my Ramadan timetable saved online?+

No. Calculations run in your browser. Shared location and method preferences may be stored locally, while calendar and CSV downloads happen only when you choose them.
