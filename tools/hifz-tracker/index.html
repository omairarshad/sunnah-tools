Quran Hifz Tracker: memorisation goals, revision and a complete guide

Short answer: A Hifz planner tracks a chosen Surah's ayah range and how many verses you have personally marked as memorised. A sustainable goal combines new memorisation, recent revision and older revision. This tool validates verse ranges for a curated eleven-Surah list, stores progress locally and offers optional JSON backup; it cannot verify recitation or retention.

What does Hifz mean and how is it different from reading?

Hifz commonly refers to memorising the Quran accurately. Reading a verse once, understanding its meaning, recognising a page, and reliably reciting it from memory are not identical achievements. Sahih al-Bukhari 5027 commends learning and teaching the Quran. A digital completion count records your self-assessment and cannot replace qualified teacher feedback.

Why the tool checks Surah and Ayah boundaries

Verse counts vary between Quranic Surahs. A goal ending at ayah 40 is appropriate for An-Naba but invalid for Al-Ikhlas, which has four ayahs. The new tool provides a curated list of eleven Surahs with fixed verified ayah totals. It rejects invalid end verses and completed counts larger than the actual range, rather than quietly treating any number up to 9999 as Quranic content. Other Surahs are intentionally not available until their reference data is reviewed.

How to plan Hifz step by step

  1. Choose one supported Surah and read the exact verses at Quran.com or in a trusted Mushaf.
  2. Enter a first and last ayah within the Surah's checked length.
  3. Set the number of ayahs already memorised within that range.
  4. Choose an achievable number of days for the remaining verses.
  5. Use the suggested daily pace as arithmetic, not as a religious requirement.
  6. Increase your count only when you judge that you can recite those verses from memory, and schedule independent revision.
  7. Download a local backup when transferring devices or making major changes.

New memorisation, recent review and older review

A practical programme separates sabaq (new memorisation), review of recent passages, and consolidation of older passages; teaching terminology varies. The counter only measures newly completed verses within one target and does not certify whether you can retain them after days or months. Revise consistently, recite to a teacher and correct mistakes rather than racing through an arbitrary count.

Worked Hifz example: Surah Al-Mulk

Surah Al-Mulk (67) contains 30 ayahs. Suppose you choose verses 1–30, mark 12 as memorised, and want to work through the remaining 18 over nine days. The tool suggests 18 ÷ 9 = 2 new ayahs per day, with the next uncompleted ayah being 67:13.

This does not imply that 2 ayahs are appropriate for every reader or that a first successful recall proves lasting Hifz. A revision and Tajwid schedule should be arranged separately.

How Hifz progress and backups are stored

Progress is saved to local browser storage, not an external account. Valid compatible old plans from the earlier tracker can migrate when they clearly match a supported Surah; incompatible legacy data remains untouched rather than silently reinterpreted. JSON exports contain the selected Surah and personal progress. Import validates the version, ayah bounds, days and count, and confirmation is required before replacement.

Common Hifz planning mistakes to avoid

Do not mark ayahs memorised merely because they were read, mislabel a Surah, enter a range beyond its last ayah, or assume that all verses require the same amount of time. Avoid making the progress percentage a statement of spiritual rank. Revision is essential when moving from short Surahs to longer passages. See Sahih al-Bukhari 5033 on keeping the Quran refreshed in memory.

Hifz, Quran Planner and Juz reading: choosing the right tool

The Juz Finder locates reading-part boundaries. The Quran Planner lays out pages across days. The Hifz Tracker focuses on memory within a selected Surah; neither page reading nor the thirty-Juz division proves memorisation accuracy.

Frequently asked questions about hifz tracker

What does Hifz mean?

Memorising the Quran, with accurate recitation and retention.

Does the app verify my Tajwid?

No. A qualified teacher or trusted recitation assessment is still needed.

Can I choose any Surah?

The current version supports eleven curated Surahs with checked verse counts.

Can the tracker accept an invalid Ayah number?

It rejects ranges that exceed the selected Surah's known final ayah.

Can I set a daily goal?

Yes. The tool calculates a mathematical target from the remaining ayahs and selected days.

Will it remember my progress?

Yes, in the current browser's local storage.

Can I restore an old Hifz plan?

Compatible old plans for supported Surahs migrate; unsupported old entries stay in their previous storage.

Does it make up my revision timetable?

No. New memorisation and revision need independent planning.

Can I export a backup?

Yes, as a small validated JSON file.

What is the next Ayah in my plan?

The tracker links to the first verse beyond the number you marked complete.

Can I print my Hifz progress?

Yes, using your browser's print controls.

How do I avoid forgetting verses?

Use consistent revision and qualified feedback; reported teachings emphasise reviewing memorisation.

How to use Hifz Memorisation Tracker

Plan an achievable memorisation range and track the verses you have reviewed or memorised over time.

The tracker uses your own entered goal and progress, then estimates the remaining verses per day for a chosen number of days.

Example: Record a small verse target and schedule revision before adding new material.

How it works

The tool divides the outstanding verse count by your target days and stores your entered values on your device.

What to check

A numerical goal does not measure recitation quality or mastery. Review memorisation with a qualified teacher and use appropriate revision.

The number completed does not measure retention or recitation quality.

Common questions

Does it verify that my recitation is accurate?+

No. The tracker only records progress; recitation should be reviewed with a capable teacher.

Can I keep my Hifz progress without an account?+

Yes. The tracker stores the current plan on this device, so clear-browser-data actions may remove it.

Does a verse count mean I have mastered a Surah?+

No. Memorisation and revision need careful listening, repetition and correction with a teacher or reliable reciter.

Should I memorise a fixed number of verses every day?+

Choose a sustainable target that includes revision. This tracker estimates progress but does not assess Tajwid, fluency or memorisation accuracy.
