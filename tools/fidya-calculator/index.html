What is Fidya in Islam, and is it the same as a missed-fast fee?

Fidya commonly describes compensatory feeding in particular fasting circumstances. Quran 2:184 contains the food-compensation language, while the adjoining verse describes Ramadan fasting and replacement days after illness or travel. Treating every missed day as an automatic cash fee collapses legally different situations into one calculation and can be misleading.

Sahih al-Bukhari 4505 records Ibn Abbas explaining the verse with reference to elderly people who lack the strength to fast, feeding one needy person for each day. That source illustrates the lasting-inability context. It is not a general rule that everyone who misses a Ramadan fast may replace it with a payment. A date, diagnosis, age or browser selection does not create a valid religious verdict by itself.

Who may owe Fidya, and when is Qada a different path?

Cases of long-term inability to fast are commonly considered for Fidya. By contrast, the Quran explicitly addresses making up days after particular temporary reasons, including illness or travel. Pregnancy, breastfeeding, menstruation, recovery, later ability to fast, delayed Qada and differing schools of law raise further questions. There may be case-specific disagreement about whether feeding alone, Qada alone or an additional remedy is relevant.

Ask a qualified scholar or your recognised authority which days actually qualify, the proper feeding method, recipient criteria and whether currency substitution is allowed. For ongoing health limitations, discuss fasting safety with an appropriate healthcare professional as well. The tool only calculates a number once you supply those externally verified decisions.

For fasts that you have been advised to make up, use the private Fasting Tracker. For a broken oath or a particular Ramadan violation, use the Kaffarah guide rather than treating it as Fidya.

How to calculate a conditional Fidya estimate step by step

  1. Establish eligibility outside the app. Do not assume all missed fasts qualify.
  2. Count applicable days for each person. This version supports up to twelve anonymous entries, rather than collecting names or family details.
  3. Obtain a current local feeding figure. Use a qualified mosque, scholar or trusted charity that explains the basis and delivery. No universal UK, US or worldwide rate is assumed.
  4. Select the currency. Currency changes the display label; it does not convert an amount between currencies.
  5. Review the formula. Each person’s confirmed eligible days are multiplied by the entered rate, then combined.
  6. Export or print if useful. A labelled, conditional summary is generated locally. No payment is processed.

The cost you enter might reflect an individual meal, a food package or another rate chosen under a particular local ruling. This tool deliberately does not decide the required quantity of food, number of meals, whether a single person may receive several days' compensation or whether funds can replace a food distribution. Ask before relying on the calculation.

Worked Fidya example: two people, ten eligible days

Hypothetical only: Person 1 has 7 days independently confirmed as Fidya-eligible. Person 2 has 3. An invented example rate of £6 per eligible day produces Person 1: 7 × £6 = £42; Person 2: 3 × £6 = £18; combined: £60.

This is arithmetic for a worked example, not the actual UK Fidya rate, a ruling that either person owes Fidya, or confirmation that a cash payment would fulfil an obligation. Replace the rate with a locally verified amount before planning anything real.

If person 2 instead needs Qada rather than Fidya, their three days should not be included in the Fidya calculation; moving numbers between tools does not determine eligibility. Likewise, a change in the local rate changes the total proportionately without changing the religious category.

Fidya, Qada, Kaffarah and Fitrana are not the same

Qada fasts

Make-up fasting on other days in applicable cases. See the private Qada tracker.

Fidya

Feeding-related compensation in eligible fasting circumstances, with the method and rate checked locally.

Kaffarah

Separate expiations for specified events. An oath can involve ten needy people, while a particular Ramadan report concerns sixty.

Fitrana / Zakat al-Fitr

Distinct giving at the end of Ramadan, with separate conditions. See the Fitrana Calculator.

Do not apply an amount quoted by a charity under one category to another. Check which religious obligation is relevant, the local basis of any price, and the charity’s delivery policy. Sunnah Tools has no live charity-rate feed or payment facility in this calculator.

What happens to your Fidya calculator data?

All arithmetic runs inside the browser. We do not save the days or rates you enter in local storage, transmit the entered values to an account or collect payments. The optional text download lists your entered anonymous rows and total, with a clear warning that eligibility and method are unverified. Print / Save PDF is the browser’s own print function.

If you share or keep a downloaded file, that copy is your responsibility; it may contain figures you consider personal. The calculator’s default is blank, and its worked example is labelled hypothetical rather than presented as a current market rate.

Fidya calculator and eligibility FAQs

What is Fidya for missed Ramadan fasts?

Fidya refers to feeding-related compensation in particular circumstances under Quran 2:184 and the relevant rulings. It is not an automatic fee for everyone who misses or breaks a fast.

Who is eligible for Fidya instead of making up fasts?

Cases of lasting inability to fast are commonly discussed in this context. Sahih al-Bukhari 4505 reports Ibn Abbas explaining elderly people without strength to fast. Temporary illness, pregnancy, nursing and other cases need individual qualified guidance.

How is Fidya calculated?

After eligibility and a local per-day feeding amount are independently confirmed, the arithmetic is eligible person-days multiplied by that rate. The calculator does not establish either input.

How much Fidya is one day in the UK?

There is no reliable permanent UK amount for every provider or interpretation. Enter a currently confirmed rate from your chosen qualified charity, mosque or scholar; the site does not invent or retrieve a live rate.

Do I pay Fidya and make up a fast?

Different circumstances and schools can require different treatments, including discussions of delayed make-up days. Do not assume this calculator answers whether one or both applies.

Can Fidya cover multiple members of a family?

You can enter up to twelve anonymous people, each with a separately established number of eligible days, and apply a single entered local daily rate for transparent arithmetic.

Is Fidya the same as Zakat al-Fitr or Fitrana?

No. Fitrana is associated with the end of Ramadan, while Fidya here relates to particular fasting cases. Zakat and Kaffarah are also separate categories.

What is the difference between Fidya and Kaffarah?

Fidya is feeding-related compensation in specific fasting situations; Kaffarah concerns distinct expiation cases such as a broken deliberate oath or particular Ramadan violations. The rules and recipients are not interchangeable by default.

Can I just pay money instead of supplying food?

Whether cash payment, food distribution, amounts and recipient criteria are permissible depends on the authority and legal school you follow. A currency total is an estimate, not a statement about payment validity.

Does the calculator send my amounts to a charity?

No. It does not collect donations, transmit an order or store personal financial inputs. You must independently verify any organisation before making a donation.

Can I print or download the calculation?

Yes. You can download a text breakdown or use your browser Print / Save PDF. Both are produced locally and include an explicit eligibility caveat.

What if someone may be able to fast in the future?

Do not choose Fidya solely because fasting is difficult right now. The distinction between temporary and lasting inability is important; seek qualified religious and appropriate medical guidance.
