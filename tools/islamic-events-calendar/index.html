Islamic Events Calendar: Ramadan, both Eids, Ashura and Hajj dates

Short answer: The Islamic calendar is lunar. Observances such as Ramadan, Eid al-Fitr, Eid al-Adha and the day of Arafah fall on Hijri dates, but their equivalent Gregorian days vary by year, locality and moon-sighting announcements. This page provides labelled calculated Umm al-Qura civil-date estimates, not confirmed festival declarations.

Which major Islamic dates appear in the calendar?

The reference index includes Islamic New Year at 1 Muharram, Ashura on 10 Muharram, the beginning of Ramadan on 1 Ramadan, Eid al-Fitr on 1 Shawwal, the beginning of Hajj rites on 8 Dhul Hijjah, the day of Arafah on 9 Dhul Hijjah, and Eid al-Adha on 10 Dhul Hijjah. These are conventional Hijri labels, not confirmed future local civil days.

Why Islamic events move through the Gregorian year

Twelve lunar months total approximately 354 or 355 days, unlike the longer Gregorian solar year. Islamic observances therefore move earlier through the seasons over successive solar years. Quran 2:189 describes lunar crescents as markers of time, and Quran 9:36 refers to twelve months.

Umm al-Qura versus local moon sighting

The calculator uses the Umm al-Qura system to suggest civil-date equivalents. Actual Ramadan and Eid observances may use local crescent sightings, an official authority's announcement or a different adopted calculation system. The optional day-shift is a transparent illustration, not a forecast or official ruling.

How to browse and export estimated events

Select the relevant Hijri year, filter all events, fasting-related dates, Hajj dates or Eids, and compare the calculated Gregorian dates with the optional illustrative local shift. Export CSV for a spreadsheet or ICS to an external calendar. The calendar export deliberately contains unshifted dates and describes them as estimates.

Worked example: following three Dhul Hijjah dates

The conventional Hajj-related sequence is 8 Dhul Hijjah for the beginning of Hajj rites, 9 Dhul Hijjah for Arafah, and 10 Dhul Hijjah for Eid al-Adha. The tool converts each numbered Hijri day to a calculated Gregorian civil date for your chosen year. Official Saudi and local announcements may differ.

Why an Islamic event can begin the previous evening

An Islamic religious day is reckoned from sunset in the relevant location. A calendar line saying 1 Ramadan on a Thursday civil date does not specify the exact moment Ramadan begins or local Taraweeh timing. Confirm the lunar month's announcement and local prayer timetable.

Can the calendar predict Laylatul Qadr?

No. Laylatul Qadr is sought especially within the last ten nights of Ramadan, and no universal fixed Gregorian date can be guaranteed for the future. A calendar that labels a particular evening as certainly Laylatul Qadr overstates its knowledge. Use the separate Laylatul Qadr planner for a cautious schedule.

Planning travel, leave and community gatherings responsibly

Treat estimates as provisional when booking nonrefundable travel, school absence, mosque events or Eid leave. Recheck official community announcements nearer the date. Exported all-day reminders are civil labels, not precise sunset notifications or religious determinations.

Common questions

Are the dates guaranteed for my country?+

No. Month starts and festivals may be announced differently locally based on moon sighting; confirm with your mosque or recognised authority.

Why do Islamic holidays move each year?+

The lunar Hijri year is shorter than the Gregorian solar year, so observances shift earlier on the Gregorian calendar.

Can I book travel using these dates?+

Use them only for planning estimates until official dates and travel arrangements are confirmed.

Do major Islamic events fall on the same Gregorian day every year?+

No. Islamic dates follow lunar months, which move through the solar year. Some observances can also differ locally because of moon-sighting conventions.
