Islamic Age Calculator: complete Hijri lunar age and birthday guide

Short answer: Islamic age is often expressed as the number of completed Hijri lunar years since a birth date. Because a lunar year lasts roughly 354–355 days, it is shorter than a Gregorian solar year and a person's numeric Hijri age is often greater. This calculator uses the named Umm al-Qura system and compares it with conventional Gregorian birthdays.

What is the difference between Hijri age and Gregorian age?

A Gregorian year follows the solar calendar and typically has 365 days (366 in leap years). An Islamic Hijri year follows twelve lunar months and normally comprises 354 or 355 days. Over many years, the shorter lunar anniversaries accumulate more completed years by the same Gregorian date. Quran 9:36 refers to twelve months, and Quran 2:189 describes lunar crescents as markers of time.

How completed Hijri years are calculated

The tool converts the Gregorian birthday and today's civil date to numbered Umm al-Qura Hijri day, month and year, then compares the latest lunar anniversary. It subtracts one year if the current year's matching Hijri birthday has not yet occurred. It likewise computes completed Gregorian years using a civil birthday comparison. Multiplying a solar age by 365/354 is only a rough estimate and can be wrong around birthdays, so the tool uses calendar dates instead.

How to calculate your age in Islamic years

  1. Enter your actual Gregorian birth date.
  2. Read the calculated Hijri equivalent of the birth date.
  3. Compare the completed Gregorian and Hijri years on today's date.
  4. Review the next calculated Hijri anniversary and any fallback from a 30-day lunar birthday to day 29.
  5. Check the local moon-sighting and sunset rules if you need a date for an actual religious or legal question.

Worked example: why a Hijri age can be higher

Imagine two people born on the same civil day who look at their ages years later. One counts completed 365/366-day Gregorian years; the other counts completed 354/355-day lunar years. Across roughly 33 Gregorian years, approximately 34 Hijri years elapse, but the exact birthdays depend on calendar month lengths and day boundaries.

That 33-versus-34 comparison is explanatory, not a calculation for a real person's birth date. Enter an actual date in the app for its precise supported-calendar comparison.

Does your Hijri birthday begin at sunset?

Islamic dates traditionally change at sunset in the relevant locality, while computers often map Umm al-Qura dates to civil Gregorian daytime labels. A person born after sunset may be treated differently in a locally observed religious calendar. The calculator has no birth time or verified local moon-sighting data, so its civil-day output should not be described as an authoritative sunset-adjusted birth date.

What if you were born on the 30th of a Hijri month?

Some later lunar years contain only 29 days in the same month. For an upcoming anniversary with no day 30, this planner labels a fallback to day 29. That is a calendar reminder convention, not a universal religious ruling on how a specific birthday or legal threshold must be reckoned. Printed calendars and local announcements can differ from Umm al-Qura calculations.

Can an Islamic age calculator determine puberty or religious obligations?

No. Puberty, responsibility, fasting eligibility, marriage law, legal age and individual religious duties are not determined simply by a numerical lunar-age output. Physical signs, school-specific fiqh and applicable law can matter. Consult qualified scholarly and legal advice for any consequential decision; this is an informational calendar tool only.

Birth-date privacy and calendar limits

The date is used locally during the current browser session and is not saved to an account or submitted to the site's server. The current version limits results to the supported Umm al-Qura range around 1300–1599 AH. For Gregorian leap-day births, the comparison uses a labelled 28 February planning convention when a non-leap year has no 29 February.
