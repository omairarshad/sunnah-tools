Short answer: Ramadan begins with the new Hijri month at local sunset. If a community expects its first daytime fast on Monday, the first Ramadan evening begins on the preceding Sunday at Maghrib. Does Ramadan start at midnight, sunset or Fajr?

In Islamic datekeeping, a new Hijri date begins at sunset. The first Ramadan night therefore precedes the first Ramadan daytime fast. Fasting itself begins at true dawn, described in Quran 2:187; it does not begin at sunset the night before.

A countdown to midnight is easy to program but does not mark the religious beginning of the month. This tool instead calculates Maghrib on the Gregorian evening before your selected first fast. The clock updates regularly while open, and after the calculated moment passes it says so rather than displaying a negative countdown.

Why is the expected first day of Ramadan uncertain?

Sahih al-Bukhari 1909 connects the beginning and ending of the Ramadan fast with the crescent and describes completing Sha'ban when the sky is obscured. Communities differ over local and wider moon-sighting criteria or calculated-calendar policies. An Umm al-Qura date is useful as a working estimate, but is not a report of a locally observed crescent.

Never read the countdown's default as a binding date for every country. Keep the expected first fast editable, and update it when the local authority you follow announces the month. The calendar's end also depends on whether Ramadan contains 29 or 30 days and the Shawwal announcement.

For a fuller explanation of calendar conventions, see the Hijri Converter, Ramadan Timetable and Ramadan dates guide.

How to use the Ramadan countdown, step by step

  1. Enter your expected first fast. Use the Gregorian date of the first daytime fast, not the evening of a moon-sighting announcement.
  2. Choose your location. Search a city or enter both coordinates and its correct IANA timezone.
  3. Check the method. A calculated Maghrib is an astronomical estimate, and special polar/high-latitude treatment requires local guidance.
  4. Read the two events. The countdown targets the preceding sunset; the next morning’s Fajr is displayed separately when available.
  5. Recheck the announcement. Use the earlier/later date scenarios only as possibilities, then set the real date when confirmed.
  6. Export responsibly. An .ics event contains the calculated sunset instant, but will not automatically update after an announcement.

Worked example: first fast on 8 February 2027

Suppose a person types Monday, 8 February 2027 as an illustrative first fasting day. The countdown targets the calculated sunset of Sunday, 7 February 2027 at the chosen location. The first daytime fast begins at Fajr on 8 February.

We deliberately do not assign a universal sunset clock time here: Maghrib varies by city, timezone and local horizon. This example is not an official 2027 moon-sighting announcement.

If the community later announces the first fast on 9 February, the relevant first evening moves to sunset on 8 February. Update the date in the tool; an earlier calendar download must be replaced manually.

How does a Ramadan countdown handle location, DST and high latitudes?

The underlying Adhan JS library calculates prayer-time instants from geographical coordinates and method parameters. The countdown uses the resulting absolute UTC sunset instant and formats it in your selected IANA timezone. This avoids assuming your device uses the same clocks as Makkah, London or another chosen city.

The countdown measures elapsed time to the instant, so daylight-saving transitions are naturally included. A printed local wall time can change under BST or other regional rules, but the UTC instant remains definite for the chosen settings.

A calculated sunset is not a direct horizon observation. At polar latitudes and in unusual twilight, an approximation may be unavailable or religiously disputed. The tool displays an unavailable result if it cannot establish a valid local sunset; it does not invent a substitute.

What should I prepare before the first Ramadan fast?

  • Confirm the announced month start and identify the first daytime fasting date.
  • Check the Suhoor and Iftar times for the location where you will actually fast, using the method followed by your mosque.
  • Review the difference between Fajr and an optional earlier imsak preparation reminder. The actual daily fast is tied to true dawn.
  • Prepare a manageable plan for Quran reading, charity, prayer and rest without treating a personally chosen target as a compulsory religious quota. The Quran Planner can help.
  • Use the last ten nights planner later in Ramadan, and recheck the length of the month as Shawwal approaches.

Ramadan start and countdown FAQs

When does Ramadan begin: at sunset or midnight?

The Islamic date changes at sunset. If Monday is the first Gregorian fasting day, Ramadan begins on the preceding Sunday evening after local sunset, not at Monday midnight.

Is the displayed Ramadan start date official?

No. The initial Umm al-Qura date is algorithmic and the sunset is calculated. A local crescent announcement may confirm or change the first fast date.

Why do Ramadan countdown websites show different numbers?

They may count to midnight, to the first Fajr or to the preceding Maghrib, and may use different proposed start dates, cities or timezones. This page labels its exact sunset target.

Why does the countdown change when I choose another city?

Sunset occurs at different UTC instants around the world. The display also uses the city’s timezone, including daylight-saving rules.

What if the local announcement is one day later?

Change the first fasting day or select the later scenario. Regenerate a downloaded event if you previously added one to your calendar.

Does reaching zero prove Ramadan has begun?

No. Reaching zero means the selected calculated sunset has passed. The tool does not observe the crescent or establish which date your religious authority follows.

Can I use the countdown at a high latitude?

Only as a transparent estimate when the astronomical calculation yields a valid sunset. Consult your local community for polar or unusual twilight conditions.

What date should I enter for the first fasting day?

Use the Gregorian daytime date of the first fast, as confirmed or provisionally expected by your community. Do not enter the previous evening’s crescent announcement date.

Does the countdown automatically track moon sightings?

No. No live moon-sighting or official-telegram feed is connected. The user can edit the date when a trusted announcement becomes available.

Are my location and chosen date stored online?

No. This countdown runs in your browser, and it does not record a personal fasting history or create an account. Calendar export occurs only when selected.
