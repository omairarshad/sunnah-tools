White Days fasting calendar: 13, 14 and 15 Hijri explained

Short answer: The White Days, or Ayyam al-Bid, are the 13th, 14th and 15th days of each Islamic lunar month. Fasting them is a recommended voluntary practice outside months and dates with separate rules. Their Gregorian dates must be converted from the Hijri calendar and checked against local moon-sighting announcements.

What are Ayyam al-Bid, and why are they called White Days?

White Days is the English name for al-Ayyam al-Bid, associated with the bright moonlit nights around the middle of a lunar month. They refer to the 13th, 14th and 15th Hijri daytime dates often recommended for fasting, not to the 13th–15th of each Gregorian month.

A Hijri date begins at sunset on the preceding civil evening. The fast itself is observed during the daytime of Hijri 13, 14 or 15, from true dawn to sunset. A calendar app that displays only a Gregorian date is a planning aid, not a direct measurement of lunar visibility.

What is the authentic evidence for fasting three days a month?

Sunan Abi Dawud 2449 reports encouragement to fast the thirteenth, fourteenth and fifteenth days of the month; Sunnah.com displays a Sahih grading attributed to al-Albani. A broader report in Sahih Muslim 1160 describes fasting three days each month without making one particular set of days the exclusive possibility.

These sources should be read in their contexts. For ordinary months, the White Days pattern is an optional practice. It does not automatically override a separate prohibition, obligation, personal exemption or applicable school-specific fasting guidance.

White Days during Ramadan and the days of Tashreeq

Ramadan 13, 14 and 15

These dates occur within obligatory Ramadan fasting, addressed in Quran 2:185. They are not a separate set of additional voluntary fasts.

Dhul Hijjah 13

The 11th, 12th and 13th days of Dhul Hijjah are the days of Tashreeq. Sahih Muslim 1141a describes them as days of eating and drinking. An ordinary optional White Day fast is not recommended for the 13th.

The planner keeps Dhul Hijjah 14 and 15 as potential ordinary voluntary dates, but excludes the 13th from the eligible ICS download. Some Hajj-specific exceptions and individual fasting rulings require qualified religious guidance; this simple tool cannot evaluate them.

Do not use the automatically generated calendar to override an established fast, an exemption for illness or travel, or the guidance of your recognised religious authority. This is date organisation, not a personalized fiqh verdict.

Worked example: a White Days set crossing a Gregorian week

Imagine a calculated Hijri month in which the 13th falls on a Gregorian Monday. The 14th would normally be Tuesday and the 15th Wednesday, unless a calendar adjustment is applied. If the community's month start is established one civil day later than the computational calendar, their corresponding dates may move to Tuesday, Wednesday and Thursday.

These weekday examples are hypothetical; they do not designate a particular month's verified White Days. A one-day toggle in the planner only previews such a difference.

For a reference date on the Hijri 14th, the planner still shows 13, 14 and 15; the 13th is marked as passed. For a reference after the 15th, it begins with the next Hijri month so the forward plan stays useful.

White Days frequently asked questions

What are the White Days in Islam?

Ayyam al-Bid commonly refers to the 13th, 14th and 15th days of a Hijri lunar month. Their association with the moon’s brighter nights gives them their name.

Do White Days fall on Gregorian dates 13, 14 and 15?

No. The dates are 13–15 in the Islamic lunar calendar. Their Gregorian dates move through the year and may differ by moon-sighting convention.

Is fasting the White Days compulsory?

Outside Ramadan they are generally treated as recommended voluntary fasting days, not an obligation. The separate duty to fast Ramadan is not a second optional White Days fast.

Can I fast any three days in a Hijri month instead?

Sahih Muslim 1160 reports the practice of three monthly fasts without insisting on particular days. The White Days are a named recommended pattern, not the only approach to voluntary monthly fasting.

Can I fast on 13 Dhul Hijjah?

The 13th of Dhul Hijjah is one of the days of Tashreeq, not ordinarily a voluntary fasting day. Specific Hajj-related circumstances may have separate rulings requiring qualified guidance.

What happens to White Days during Ramadan?

The Hijri dates 13–15 still occur, but their fasts are part of the obligatory Ramadan fast, not an additional voluntary practice. The planner labels them accordingly.

Why does a local mosque list a White Day one day later?

Computed Umm al-Qura calendars and local crescent announcements can disagree. Use the illustrative ±1 display for comparison only, and follow the locally recognised calendar.

Why are some dates shown as passed?

If the selected reference is still before the end of a month’s White Days, the tool retains all three dates and marks earlier ones as passed. After the 15th, it begins with the next Hijri month.

Will a calendar import notify me at Fajr?

No. The ICS export uses all-day Gregorian date markers for potential voluntary days. Find true-dawn and sunset times separately for your location.

Does the .ics file contain Ramadan or Tashreeq fast recommendations?

No. The eligible-date ICS excludes Ramadan and the 13th of Dhul Hijjah. The full CSV includes all three days of every month with explanatory status labels.

Can I print a 12-month White Days calendar?

Yes. Choose 12 months, confirm the displayed calendar convention, and use Print / Save PDF. The dates remain estimates until checked locally.

Does the planner save my personal fasting activity?

No. It generates dates and voluntary calendar downloads in the browser; it does not record completed or missed fasts or synchronise a personal account.

Common questions

Are White Days always voluntary fasts on 13, 14 and 15?+

They are ordinarily recommended in non-Ramadan months, but Ramadan is an obligatory fast and the 13th of Dhul Hijjah is Tashreeq rather than an ordinary optional fast. Check your local guidance.

Which Hijri dates are the White Days?+

The 13th, 14th and 15th of each lunar Hijri month, not the corresponding dates of the Gregorian month.

Is White Days fasting compulsory?+

It is ordinarily a recommended voluntary practice outside Ramadan and dates with separate rulings. The calendar does not decide personal eligibility.

Can I fast any three days of the month?+

Sahih Muslim 1160 discusses monthly three-day fasting without making a specific set of days exclusive; White Days form a named recommended pattern.

Should I fast on 13 Dhul Hijjah?+

It is a day of Tashreeq, not an ordinary voluntary fast. Specific Hajj-related circumstances may need separate qualified guidance.

What about White Days during Ramadan?+

The 13th–15th remain Hijri calendar dates, but those fasts belong to obligatory Ramadan fasting, not an additional optional routine.

Why do White Days move across Gregorian dates?+

Hijri months follow a lunar calendar and contain 29 or 30 days. Local sighting conventions can also shift corresponding civil dates.

What does the ±1 day selector mean?+

It displays neighbouring Gregorian dates for comparison and does not confirm a local moon sighting or decree the correct calendar.

Can I download 12 months as a CSV?+

Yes. The full CSV includes each month’s three dates and labels for Ramadan, Tashreeq, past and potential voluntary days.

Does the .ics file include prohibited or obligatory fasts?+

It exports only upcoming dates labelled potential ordinary voluntary days; Ramadan and Dhul Hijjah 13 are excluded.

Does the calendar tell me Fajr and iftar times?+

No. It produces all-day date markers. Use Suhoor & Iftar Times for local dawn and sunset estimates.
