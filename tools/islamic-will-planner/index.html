Islamic Will Preparation Planner

Prepare confidential notes for assets, debts, heirs, guardians, charitable intentions and questions for a UK solicitor and Islamic inheritance expert.

Islamic will planning in the UK

A will helps you prepare for family responsibilities, debts and charitable wishes. Islamic rules about heirs and bequests and national legal requirements must both be considered.

Why this is a preparation worksheet

Our form only organises topics you might discuss with experts: assets, liabilities, potential heirs, guardians, executor, charitable wishes and records. It does not produce legally binding wording or distribute the estate.

Legal requirements in England and Wales

A legally valid will usually requires the testator’s signature in the presence of two adult witnesses, who also sign in the testator’s presence. Witnesses and their spouses generally cannot benefit under that will. Scotland and Northern Ireland have different requirements.

Islamic shares and bequests

Quran 4:11–12 and 4:176 discuss prescribed inheritance portions. Specific cases depend on surviving relatives, debts and other details. Classical bequest rules often restrict gifts to non-heirs to one-third unless appropriate heirs consent, with significant legal and fiqh subtleties. Get qualified advice rather than calculating from an incomplete family list.

01Two distinct layers: religious distribution and civil law+

Islamic inheritance rules concern debts, bequests and prescribed heirs. A will must also satisfy the law of the place where it takes effect. A handwritten wish list or unexecuted computer download should not be represented as legally binding.

The worksheet intentionally does not compute shares, invent an executor, offer model legal clauses or ask for bank account numbers.

GOV.UK – Making a will↗

02Islamic heirs and the estate+

Quran 4:11–12 and 4:176 discuss specified inheritance portions. A precise distribution depends on who survives the deceased, their relationships and other legal and juristic factors. Assets, valid debts and any permissible bequests may also affect the available estate.

A simplified percentage calculator can mislead when family relationships, surviving spouses, parents or other heirs are missing. Have the complete circumstances reviewed by a qualified Islamic inheritance specialist.

Quran 4:11 – Heir shares↗

03Wills, charitable wishes and the one-third discussion+

Classical jurisprudence distinguishes prescribed heirs from discretionary bequests, often discussing a limit of one-third for non-heir gifts and the question of consent for certain exceptions. This is not an unconditional permission to rewrite prescribed heir shares.

Record charitable intentions as topics for a specialist rather than specifying percentages that the site cannot verify.

Sahih al-Bukhari 2742 – One-third bequest discussion↗

04Signing a will in England and Wales+

GOV.UK explains that a valid will generally needs to be made voluntarily by an eligible adult of sound mind, in writing, and signed in the presence of two adult witnesses who also sign in the testator’s presence. Witness beneficiaries and their spouses are subject to restrictions.

Separate rules exist in Scotland and Northern Ireland. If you have assets outside the UK, different national laws or tax issues may apply; get appropriate cross-border advice.

GOV.UK – Make sure your will is legal↗

05Preparation checklist for a solicitor+

Make a non-sensitive inventory of types of assets and liabilities; list potential heirs and relevant family relationships; consider executors and guardians; and identify where legal records are held. Do not paste card numbers, passwords or authentication secrets into a general-purpose worksheet.

A solicitor can help distinguish assets passing under the will from pensions, jointly held property, nominated benefits and trusts. An Islamic specialist can advise how those arrangements interact with your religious wishes.

  • Location of property and other significant assets
  • Outstanding liabilities and funeral arrangements
  • Surviving relatives and possible heirs
  • Executor, guardianship and professional contacts
  • Cross-border assets or prior wills
  • Charitable intentions and a review of tax/legal effects
06Worked example: a family with a spouse and children+

A person has a spouse, dependent children, a home, savings and a pension. It may be unclear what legally forms part of their estate and which relatives qualify as heirs.

The worksheet captures the questions and documents to discuss, but it intentionally produces no percentages or inheritance verdict.

Worked example:Prepare categories of assets and debts, list family relationships, identify executors and bring questions to both a solicitor and a qualified inheritance scholar.
