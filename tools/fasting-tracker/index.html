Fasting tracker and Qada fasts: complete planning and evidence guide

Short answer: A fasting tracker helps you organise days you have already decided to make up, record completed fasts and view the remaining number. What does a fasting tracker actually measure?

The completed count is made from dates you choose to record, plus any undated completions brought over from the older Sunnah Tools tracker. Remaining Qada days is your chosen target minus recorded Qada completions. A voluntary completion never subtracts from Qada. These figures represent your own record, not a verified religious ruling, a mosque-issued statement or an audit of fasting validity.

Recording a fast involves personal intention and actual observance from true dawn until sunset. A browser can remember a date; it cannot verify intention, health circumstances, exemptions, Fajr timing or what happened during that day. Use the separate Suhoor & Iftar Times calculator for estimated local dawn and sunset.

What is Qada fasting, and when are make-up fasts needed?

Quran 2:184 and 2:185 distinguish fasting during Ramadan, making up an equivalent number on other days in specified situations, and feeding-related language. Illness and travel appear directly in the verses. Other circumstances—including menstruation, pregnancy, breastfeeding, permanent inability and an uncertain fasting history—require case-specific religious guidance rather than an automatic rule inferred by a counter.

Sahih al-Bukhari 1950 reports Aisha making up some missed Ramadan fasts during Sha'ban. It demonstrates that make-up fasting is a real, separately discussed practice. It does not establish that everyone may delay indefinitely or decide alone whether delayed days also involve feeding; schools and circumstances differ. If your target is uncertain, ask a qualified scholar to help establish a responsible number before entering it.

Some dates have separate fasting rules. For example, White Days are a voluntary pattern and should not be automatically credited to Qada. The tracker keeps the two categories separate without purporting to settle any intention or overlap question.

How to track missed and completed fasts step by step

  1. Confirm your target. Enter the count you have established using your own records and qualified guidance, not a generated religious estimate.
  2. Complete the fast. The tracker does not record planned dates as completed in advance.
  3. Select Qada or voluntary. The distinction prevents a voluntary fast from silently reducing a make-up target.
  4. Choose the Gregorian date. The app rejects future dates and duplicated completions for the same day.
  5. Review your history. Remove accidental entries and adjust the target without dropping below existing completed Qada days.
  6. Back up privately. Save a JSON file for restoration or a CSV/printed copy for your records.

If you used the old counter, the existing target and completed amount are kept as undated legacy progress. They are not assigned imaginary completion dates. You can correct that old undated number one completion at a time. Browser storage can be cleared by the user or browser; backups are opt-in and remain under your control.

Worked example: tracking twelve make-up fasts

Imagine that, after obtaining suitable guidance, someone enters a Qada target of 12 days. The older counter carried over four undated completed fasts. They record three further completed Qada dates: the total recorded Qada is now 7 and the tracker shows 5 remaining.

If they also enter two voluntary fasts, the voluntary counter becomes two while the Qada remaining figure stays 5. This is an arithmetic example, not a ruling that twelve days are owed or that any particular voluntary fast fulfils Qada.

A useful record shows what you actually know, including uncertainty. If historic dates were never recorded, leaving them undated is more accurate than retroactively inventing precise dates.

Is your fasting tracker private? Backups, CSV and device changes

This tool saves your chosen target and completion entries in the web browser's local storage. It does not register an account, send your completion dates to a Sunnah Tools server or compare your worship with anyone else's. A browser profile, shared device or downloaded backup can still be accessed by someone with access to that device; use normal device privacy precautions.

  • JSON backup: records your target, dated completions and any undated legacy total. Imported files must pass strict validation; restoring replaces your current local plan only with your confirmation.
  • CSV: provides a human-readable list of completed dates and category labels, with a separate undated row for older counters.
  • Print / Save PDF: uses your browser's print flow; no PDF is produced or stored on our servers.
  • Clear: requires confirmation because it removes the saved local plan. Export first if you need a copy.

Do not mistake privacy for permanence. Device loss, private browsing, storage restrictions or clearing cookies/site data can erase local records. Make periodic backups if your own record matters to you.

Qada versus Fidya versus Kaffarah: which tool should you use?

Qada

A make-up fast in relevant cases. This tracker records the count you establish; it does not determine the duty.

Fidya

Feeding-related compensation in specific situations; not every missed fast qualifies. See the Fidya Calculator.

Kaffarah

Distinct expiations for specified cases such as a deliberate broken oath and certain Ramadan violations. See the Kaffarah Calculator.

No simple checklist can decide eligibility, whether remedies overlap, or which school applies. If illness, pregnancy, breastfeeding or another personal circumstance is relevant, obtain individual religious guidance and appropriate medical advice when health decisions are involved.

Qada fasting tracker FAQs

What is a Qada fast?

A Qada fast is a make-up fast for an obligatory Ramadan day that was not fasted or completed in circumstances requiring replacement. Quran 2:185 mentions making up an equal number of other days for illness or travel. Individual rulings vary.

How many missed Ramadan fasts do I owe?

This tracker cannot infer that number. Enter a target only after checking your own records and guidance relevant to your circumstances. If the history is uncertain, ask a qualified scholar how to estimate it.

Are Qada fasts and voluntary fasts counted together?

No. The new tracker keeps voluntary completions separate, so a voluntary tick does not reduce the target you entered for Qada.

Can I record a fast before I have completed it?

The interface is for completed fasts. It rejects dates in the future, but cannot independently verify whether a day was completed or whether its fast is valid.

What happened to my previous Fasting Tracker count?

If a prior sunnah-fasts counter exists and the new format does not, its target and completed number are carried forward as undated Qada progress. The app does not invent historic dates.

Can I record more than one fast on a date?

No. The completion list accepts only one kind of completed fast per Gregorian civil date, avoiding double-counting a single daytime fast.

Must Qada fasting days be consecutive?

Quran 2:185 refers to an equal number of other days. Interpretive rules about continuity and particular cases need qualified guidance; a personal planner cannot decide every legal condition.

Can I delay make-up fasts until Sha’ban?

Sahih al-Bukhari 1950 describes Aisha making up Ramadan fasts in Sha’ban. Do not treat the narration as a blanket endorsement of delaying without a reason; check your circumstances and school.

Does missing a fast mean I should pay Fidya?

Not necessarily. Qada, Fidya and Kaffarah address different circumstances and are not automatic substitutes for each other. See the linked Fidya and Kaffarah guides.

What data does the tracker save?

Your target, older undated count and dated entries are held in this browser’s local storage. Nothing is uploaded to a Sunnah Tools account. You can export JSON or CSV manually.

Can I move my fasting history to another device?

Download the JSON backup and restore it on the other device. Restoring replaces the destination browser’s current tracker only after you confirm.

How do I print or export my make-up fast record?

Use Print / Save PDF, download a CSV list of dated and undated progress, or export a validated JSON backup for later restoration.

Common questions

Does a fasting tracker tell me how many Qada fasts I owe?+

No. It maintains the target you choose; eligibility, intention, timing and religious obligations require qualified guidance.

How do I track missed Ramadan fasts (Qada)?+

Set your own verified target, then record each completed make-up fast by date. The counter subtracts your dated Qada completions and any older undated count; it does not decide the target.

Can I track voluntary fasts separately?+

Yes. Choose Qada or voluntary for each completed day. Voluntary entries never reduce your Qada remaining count.

Will my old fasting counter be lost?+

No. A valid previous sunnah-fasts counter is migrated to an undated completed tally without creating fake calendar dates.

Does the fasting tracker calculate what I owe?+

No. Quran 2:185 speaks of make-up days, but the correct count, exemptions and any feeding remedies require qualified case-specific guidance.

Can I record a fast in the future?+

No. This is a log of completed days; future civil dates and duplicated date entries are rejected.

Can I download my fasting history?+

Yes. Download a private JSON backup for restoration, a labelled CSV including older undated entries, or print your current view.

Where is my fasting progress saved?+

On this device in browser local storage, with no account sync. Clearing storage or changing devices may remove it unless you export a backup.

Can I restore a JSON fasting backup?+

Yes. A backup is validated before being restored, and replacing your current local plan requires confirmation.

Do I need Fidya or Kaffarah for a missed fast?+

Not automatically. Qada, Fidya and Kaffarah apply to distinct circumstances; a qualified religious authority must assess your situation.
