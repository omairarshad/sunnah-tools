How gold purity changes Zakat valuation

A 24-karat gold price is the price per gram of essentially pure gold. A 22k bracelet contains approximately 22/24 = 91.67% fine gold by metal weight; an 18k necklace contains 18/24 = 75%. Multiply gross gold-alloy mass (excluding stones) by that ratio before applying the pure-metal price.

The interface supports 24k, 22k, 21k, 18k, 14k and 9k, plus separate items for different purities. It gives an indicative metal-content estimate, not a guaranteed resale quote. A purchase receipt may include craftsmanship, retailer margin or gemstones; those are not 24k metal. If a hallmark, stone weight or plating is unclear, have the metal assessed rather than guessing.

Is gold jewellery included in Zakat? Scholarly differences

The Hanafi school generally includes personal-use gold and silver jewellery in Zakat calculations. The Shafi'i, Maliki and Hanbali approaches can exempt customary permissible personal-use jewellery, subject to qualifications. Gold held as investment or goods for sale may have different treatment. The precise boundaries and exceptions cannot be set by choosing a karat percentage.

For a contemporary scholar-reviewed explanation, see Islamic Relief UK's guide to Zakat on jewellery. Even if personal jewellery is excluded under your guidance, that does not necessarily exempt your savings or other qualifying wealth. Use the full Zakat calculator when reviewing all asset categories.

Gold and silver Nisab and the lunar-year condition

The current UK reference weights used here are 87.48g gold and 612.36g silver. Multiplying by verified per-gram prices expresses each benchmark in money. These figures are not interchangeable with kilograms of household jewellery and are not the only scholarly conversion conventions in use. Islamic Relief UK's Nisab reference explains the two bases.

Sahih al-Bukhari 1405 reports a classical silver threshold in awaq. Modern gram equivalents derive from historical weight conversion. The selected gold or silver benchmark may produce very different eligibility results, so the tool requires you to choose a benchmark rather than silently applying the cheaper one.

Hawl refers to a lunar year relevant to most qualifying forms of wealth. The precise treatment of holdings during that year and the permitted liabilities must be assessed under qualified guidance; reaching a threshold in this calculator alone does not establish that the annual Zakat is due.

Calculate Zakat on multiple gold items step by step

  1. Decide whether the particular jewellery or investment gold must be included under your school.
  2. Separate out gems and non-gold parts. Enter the gold-alloy weight and purity for each item, not the full ornament or retail purchase price.
  3. Find a currently verified 24k price per gram for your chosen currency and date. Changing currency clears monetary inputs so you cannot accidentally relabel amounts.
  4. Add other qualifying wealth (cash or savings, for example) only if appropriate, and deduct only allowed liabilities. Do not duplicate gold already represented above.
  5. Choose the gold or silver Nisab benchmark advised for your assessment. Silver selection requires a verified silver price.
  6. Read the 2.5% calculation as conditional, check hawl and your obligations externally, then export a local summary if needed.

Worked example: mixed 22k and 18k gold

Hypothetical prices: A 30g 22k piece contains 27.5g fine gold and a 20g 18k piece contains 15g. Combined pure-gold equivalent is 42.5g. At an invented 24k price of £80 per gram, the combined indicative metal value is £3,400.

Add £1,000 of independently confirmed other qualifying assets and £100 in permitted deductions: £3,400 + £1,000 − £100 = £4,300. At an illustrative silver rate of £1.20/g, silver Nisab is £734.83, so that benchmark is exceeded. The conditional 2.5% on the combined £4,300 is £107.50. At the gold benchmark (£6,998.40), the same £4,300 would be below threshold. Those are not current metal prices or instructions to choose either rule.

The calculator also shows the gold portion separately, but the overall obligation is considered against combined qualifying net wealth, not a separate 2.5% charge for every item regardless of eligibility. Always confirm which assets, jewellery and deductions apply.

Frequently asked questions about gold Zakat

Is Zakat due on gold jewellery I wear?

Schools differ. In the Hanafi school, personal gold and silver jewellery is generally included; other schools have exemptions for customary personal-use jewellery. Check your situation before adding items.

Do diamonds and gemstones count?

Not as part of the gold metal value. Weigh only the gold portion; items held as merchandise for trade require a separate asset assessment.

Is 18k gold valued at the pure gold price?

Not at its full gross weight. At 18k, pure-gold content is 18/24 (75%) of metal weight, multiplied by the verified price for 24k gold per gram.

Can I add several rings or chains?

Yes. Each of up to twelve entries may have a different weight and karat rating, and the calculator combines fine-gold equivalents.

What if the item is plated or has uncertain purity?

Plating and mixed-material pieces require an actual gold-content assessment. Do not enter a presumed full mass of gold.

Is Zakat due only if my gold weighs over 87.48g?

Not necessarily. Total qualifying wealth, including savings and other assets, can matter; assess your selected Nisab and hawl under your school.

Why is there a silver benchmark for a gold tool?

Your Nisab assessment may use gold or silver value. The two benchmarks may lead to different threshold comparisons for combined wealth.

How are debts treated?

Only include deductions allowed by your chosen guidance, not necessarily all long-term debts or future expenses.

Can I use the gold shop purchase price?

The calculator expects a verified current pure-metal price, not your historical purchase cost or a retail price with making charges.

Is the result automatically payable?

No. It is conditional arithmetic; determine inclusion of jewellery, all qualifying assets, selected benchmark and hawl before relying on it.

How often do I pay Zakat on gold?

Zakat on qualifying wealth is generally assessed against a lunar-year (hawl) requirement. Your specific anniversary and relevant holdings need review.

Does this calculator use live gold market prices?

No. It intentionally requires you to supply current verified 24k gold and, if selected, silver prices in one currency.

How to use Gold Zakat Calculator

Estimate your gold's pure-metal value before checking Zakat eligibility.

Multiply jewellery weight by purity divided by 24, then multiply by the market price of pure gold per gram you provide.

Example: Value 22-carat jewellery at its actual gold purity and a current appropriate price.

How it works

Enter grams excluding gemstones, select the karat purity and enter the market value of pure 24-karat gold per gram in your currency. The tool uses weight × karat ÷ 24 × pure gold price, then illustrates 2.5% of that value. For actual obligations, total wealth, Nisab, hawl and your school of thought matter.

What to check

Gemstones and non-gold parts should not be counted as gold weight. Different schools of thought treat personal jewellery differently.

Whether personal jewellery is included can depend on school and ownership circumstances.

Common questions

Is Zakat due on jewellery worn for personal use?+

Scholarly schools differ. The Hanafi school generally includes personal gold jewellery, while other schools may exempt jewellery in ordinary personal use. Consult qualified local guidance.

Does 18-karat gold count as pure gold?+

No. 18k is approximately 75% gold by mass; use the calculator's purity conversion.

Is Zakat obligatory on all jewellery?+

Not universally. Views differ on personally worn jewellery; confirm your situation with a qualified scholar.

Why does jewellery purity affect the estimate?+

Carat measures the proportion of fine gold in an item. Its market value can vary with purity even when gross weight is the same.
