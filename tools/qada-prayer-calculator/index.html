What is Qada Salah and when is it required?

Qada refers to a prayer made up after its appointed time. In Sahih Muslim 684a, the Prophet ﷺ describes prayer missed through sleep or forgetfulness and praying when remembered. Schools agree on this general obligation for those cases; broader discussions of deliberately omitted prayers and exclusions differ and require qualified guidance. A calculator cannot issue a fatwa based on a number of missed days.

Scholarly differences: deliberate omission, Witr and exemptions

The four Sunni schools commonly address making up intentionally missed Fard prayers, but there are differing scholarly positions about the underlying duty and associated repentance. For a Hanafi explanation see SeekersGuidance's Qada discussion. Witr is emphasised differently between the Hanafi school and other schools; the optional Witr checkbox is not a declaration that everyone owes it. Exemptions for menstruation and other circumstances also require their own treatment.

How to estimate missed prayers without pretending certainty

  1. Ask a qualified scholar which period and prayer types actually apply to your situation.
  2. Choose an estimated number of days, approximate years or inclusive civil date range.
  3. Exclude days only if your religious guidance says those prayers were not owed.
  4. Review the default one-each-day Fajr through Isha counts and override individual types when needed.
  5. Choose whether to include Witr under your school's guidance.
  6. Track only genuinely performed make-up prayers and make a local JSON backup before resetting or moving devices.

A year is estimated with an average civil-year length, not a guarantee that five prayers were owed every day of that period. Treat an uncertain estimate explicitly as uncertain.

Planning a manageable Qada pace

The optional new pace control estimates the number of civil days needed to clear the remaining calculated target at a chosen number of make-up prayers per day. It is an organisational projection only, not a religious deadline, recommended number or permission to neglect current obligatory prayers. A change in completed counts updates the remaining projection automatically.

Worked example: 30 review days and five prayers each

If a qualified assessment identifies thirty applicable days with one of each five Fard prayers to make up, the planning target is 30 × 5 = 150 prayers. If ten have already been completed and you choose an illustrative pace of 5 per civil day, the remaining 140 prayers would take 28 planning days.

Those numbers are not a statement that anyone owes 150 prayers. Excluded days, Witr, individual target overrides, illness and historical uncertainty may change the figures; ask your school which obligations apply.

Your Qada plan and completed progress stay on your device

The tool preserves the existing versioned local plan with day/year/range inputs, overrides and completed prayer counts. JSON backup and restoration are opt-in; backup files are validated and replacement requires confirmation. Clearing site storage or switching devices can delete saved progress. Unlike the Salah Tracker, the Qada plan does not infer missed obligations from empty daily checkboxes.

Qada versus prayer-time reminders and the daily Salah tracker

A prayer timetable tells you when today's prayer window begins; the Prayer Times tool provides calculated start times. The Salah Tracker records voluntary checkmarks and does not create liabilities. The Qada calculator holds only an estimate of a religiously reviewed past target and your own progress. These three functions should not be conflated.

What to check before relying on a Qada estimate

Verify the religious date at which prayer became obligatory for the person, any genuinely exempt periods, circumstances of missing the prayers, Witr requirements, and the school's approach to making them up. Also consider practical health and ability; a plan's arithmetic cannot determine someone's capacity. Ensure a realistic pace does not displace current obligations and consult an appropriate qualified teacher for complex cases.

What does Qada mean?

Making up a prayer after its prescribed time in cases where it is required.

Must missed prayers through sleep be made up?

Yes. Sahih Muslim 684a directs praying on remembering such a prayer.

What about intentionally missed prayers?

Scholarly views differ; seek guidance from your legal school.

How to use Missed Salah (Qada) Calculator

Create a private plan for potentially missed Salah, with editable prayer targets and saved on-device progress.

Choose a number of days, approximate years or an inclusive start and end date. Exclude days only after checking applicable exemptions and adjust Fajr, Dhuhr, Asr, Maghrib and Isha individually. How it works

Estimate the number of days from a supplied duration or inclusive civil date range, deduct user-selected excluded days and allow editable targets for Fajr through Isha. Witr is an optional extra, and local browser storage saves completion counts, not a synchronised account record.

What to check

This is a planning estimate rather than a determination of religious liability. Rulings differ regarding deliberately missed prayers, exemption periods, Witr and how to make up prayers. Only enter obligations established through qualified guidance.

Common questions

Can this calculate precisely how many prayers I owe?+

No. It estimates a starting period only and cannot determine rulings, valid exemptions or which missed prayers must be made up.

How do I calculate years of missed Salah?+

Select approximate years and then review the estimated number of days. Adjust the target for each prayer to reflect your circumstances, rather than treating the estimate as a ruling.

Should I include Witr when counting missed prayers?+

Witr is treated differently across schools of law. The option is off by default; include it only if it fits the guidance you follow.

Does the Qada tracker save my prayer records?+

It stores completed counts in local storage on that browser. There is no account or synchronisation, and clearing browser data may erase progress.

Does every make-up prayer plan have to include Witr?+

Not necessarily. Different schools classify Witr and its make-up differently. Enable Witr only if it matches guidance you have chosen to follow.
