Hijama Dates and Lunar Calendar

Find upcoming Hijama lunar date references, 17, 19 and 21 Hijri, with Gregorian dates and calendar exports. Includes hadith caveats and wet cupping safety.

What are Hijama dates?

Hijama is a form of cupping. Some narrations mention the 17th, 19th and 21st days of a lunar month. Our planner shows those dates using a calculated Hijri calendar; it is not a medical scheduling recommendation.

Date method

We convert calculated Umm al-Qura Hijri dates into Gregorian days. The Islamic day begins at sunset, so an all-day date reminder cannot settle the exact local sunset boundary. Different moon-sighting authorities may report a date one day earlier or later.

Hadith evidence

Sunan Abi Dawud 3861 reports these dates. Its chain and the overall strength of recommendations about particular weekdays have been discussed by hadith specialists. Avoid declaring a universal medical benefit or making a specific day religiously mandatory.

Health and safety

Evidence for benefits of cupping remains limited, particularly for conditions beyond pain. Wet cupping punctures the skin and may cause bleeding, infection or other complications. Avoid self-treatment and seek medical advice before an invasive procedure, particularly with medication, anaemia or clotting concerns.

01Wet and dry cupping are different procedures+

Dry cupping places a cup on intact skin to produce suction. Wet cupping involves breaking the skin before or during suction, exposing the person to additional bleeding and infection risks. The two approaches should not be treated as interchangeable when assessing evidence.

Cupping is practised in different cultures and sometimes called Hijama in Islamic contexts. The date planner does not choose a treatment, location on the body, practitioner or indication.

  • Dry cupping: suction without skin puncture
  • Wet cupping: involves skin puncture and blood exposure
  • Neither replaces established medical investigation or treatment

02What is actually reported about 17, 19 and 21?+

Sunan Abi Dawud 3861 reports a connection between cupping and the 17th, 19th and 21st of a lunar month. Sunnah.com lists an assessment of Hasan from al-Albani for that report. Assessments of related date and weekday narrations differ, so this planner deliberately avoids presenting one universal ruling.

A report in a hadith compilation is not equivalent to a modern clinical trial. A phrase about healing in a narration cannot establish that an invasive procedure cures all diseases.

03How the Hijri conversion works+

The calculator uses the Umm al-Qura calendar as an estimate, mapping each 17th, 19th and 21st Hijri day onto its corresponding Gregorian date. Select 3, 6 or 12 months ahead; a one-day adjustment shows a nearby possibility rather than verified local moon-sighting data.

Remember that an Islamic date begins at local sunset, whereas exported calendar reminders are all-day Gregorian events. A calendar export cannot identify the start of a lunar day at your location.

Worked example:If 17 Safar is calculated as 28 August, a local moon-sighting difference could place the equivalent civil date on 27 or 29 August. Neither alternative can be verified just by pressing the adjustment control.

04What research can and cannot establish+

NCCIH reports that many studies are low quality. There is limited evidence of possible benefit for pain, with insufficient high-quality research for other conditions. Cupping should not be advertised as effective for infertility, chronic infections, hypertension, detoxification or any particular disease without reliable condition-specific evidence.

Temporary marks and skin irritation are common considerations. Other harms include infection, burns or scars, and bloodborne infections from inadequately sterilised equipment.

05Practical safety questions before booking+

Ask the practitioner to explain their training, infection-control procedures, single-use sterile equipment, handling of blood and sharps, aftercare and what symptoms warrant medical help.

Tell a clinician about medicines that affect bleeding, a history of fainting, anaemia, clotting disorders, pregnancy or skin conditions before invasive cupping. A professional should also discuss whether any planned procedure is appropriate at all.

  • Are sterile single-use instruments used?
  • What qualifications and infection-control protocols apply?
  • What adverse effects and aftercare should I know about?
  • Should I consult my GP or treating clinician first?
06Do I have to wait for a particular date?+

The tool does not determine a religious obligation or medical need to wait until 17, 19 or 21. If you are seeking medical evaluation for pain, swelling, severe symptoms or another condition, do not delay appropriate care while waiting for a lunar date.

The safest use of this planner is learning which dates a cited narration references, not deciding whether or when you should undergo an invasive intervention.

Further questions

Is Hijama automatically safe because it is traditional?

No. Wet cupping breaks the skin and introduces specific safety risks. Traditional use is not proof of safety for every person.

Are Mondays or other weekdays forbidden?

Reports about particular weekdays have varying assessments. This website does not declare a weekday prohibited.

Can Hijama replace migraine medication or an assessment for swelling?

No. Seek clinical advice for persistent or concerning symptoms and do not suspend prescribed treatment because of the calendar.
