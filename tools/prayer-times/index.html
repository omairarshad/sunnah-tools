Understanding the five daily prayer times

Find the time your prayer begins, understand why different mosques publish different timetables, and learn how the five windows relate to dawn, the Sun and twilight. This guide explains the calculator above rather than replacing your mosque's local timetable.

Prayer begins when its window opens, which is what the calculator estimates. Jama'ah / iqamah is the later congregation time set by each mosque. Sunrise marks the end of the Fajr window; it is not a sixth obligatory prayer.

When does each prayer begin and end?

The five daily prayers have appointed periods (Quran 4:103). The table describes the major solar markers; there are additional differences of interpretation about some boundaries, especially Asr and Isha.

Fajr

The prayer window ends when the Sun begins rising. Sunrise itself is shown as a boundary, not as a sixth obligatory prayer.

Dhuhr

The clock time depends on longitude, date and the solar-noon calculation. Local mosques may add a precautionary interval.

Asr

The exact limits and late-prayer considerations require scholarly detail. Do not treat the sunset boundary as a reason to delay intentionally.

Maghrib

Maghrib starts at sunset. In Ramadan, it is also the ordinary reference for iftar.

Isha

Isha is not simply a universal fixed number of minutes after Maghrib. Umm al-Qura uses a fixed interval for its calculation, with a Ramadan variation in the convention.

The limits of the prayers are discussed in Sahih Muslim 612a. The narration also underscores that not every prayer's preferred or permitted end can be reduced to a single clock figure.

Why Fajr and Isha differ between apps and mosques

At dawn and dusk the Sun is still below the horizon. Many prayer timetables estimate the start of Fajr or Isha from the Sun's angle below the horizon. A larger Fajr angle generally produces an earlier dawn estimate. Different Isha angles and fixed-interval methods can produce noticeably different evening times.

Which method should I choose?

Start with the method published by the mosque or religious authority you follow. Check the selected city, date, time zone and Asr setting first. Then use Compare calculation methods above to see how another convention would affect Fajr and Isha on the same day. When a high-latitude rule limits twilight times, both methods may even return the same time despite having different angles. A displayed difference does not by itself prove that either authority is wrong.

Standard and Hanafi Asr — why are there two times?

Standard Asr

Uses a shadow length equivalent to the object's length in addition to its shadow at solar noon. This convention is associated with Shafi'i, Maliki and Hanbali calculations.

Hanafi Asr

Uses a shadow length equivalent to twice the object's length in addition to its noon shadow. This gives a later calculated Asr start.

Choose the Asr school followed by your mosque. Changing this option does not require changing your Fajr/Isha twilight convention. Details of scholarly interpretation go beyond a numerical timetable; compare the PrayTimes Asr rules.

UK summer, Scotland and very high latitudes

In northern Britain and other high-latitude regions, the Sun may not reach the usual twilight angle on some summer nights. A normal Fajr or Isha calculation can therefore become impractical or undefined. Apps use different approximation rules for these conditions.

  • Seventh of the night: Uses a seventh of the sunset-to-sunrise interval near dawn or dusk.
  • Middle of the night: Uses the midpoint of that overnight interval as an alternative limit.
  • Twilight-angle fraction: Uses the selected Fajr and Isha angles to form an appropriate portion of the night.
  • Polar regions: When sunrise or sunset itself is absent, another special fallback may be needed. The calculator leaves such results unavailable unless you explicitly choose a fallback.

Our calculator offers these settings under High-latitude and polar calculation settings. Its default uses the Adhan library's location-based recommended rule, but that is not a declaration that the method matches the religious authority in your area. In Newcastle, Glasgow, Edinburgh, Aberdeen and further north, check your local mosque's summer timetable before relying on a calculated Fajr or Isha estimate.

How to compare your mosque's timetable

  1. Match the location and date. Even nearby towns may have slightly different solar times, and daylight-saving changes affect the clock.
  2. Look for the column heading. A mosque may publish separate times for the prayer beginning, the adhan and the iqamah / jama'ah. Compare like with like.
  3. Check the calculation convention. Compare Fajr and Isha angles or fixed-interval settings with those displayed by the calculator.
  4. Check the Asr school. A standard/Hanafi mismatch can explain a large part of the afternoon difference.
  5. Check seasonal and safety adjustments. Local authorities may use special Ramadan, high-latitude or precautionary timetables rather than a generic app preset.
  6. Use the mosque's announcement for congregational prayer. Our calculator does not obtain or validate live mosque iqamah times.

How do time zones and daylight saving affect the result?

A proper solar calculation returns an instant in time; the selected location's time zone determines its wall-clock display. For example, Europe/London changes between GMT and BST. Do not manually add an hour to an already correctly formatted result. Travellers should choose the location where they will actually pray.

Are sunrise and Jumu'ah included?

Sunrise is provided as the end marker for Fajr, not another one of the five obligatory prayers. Friday Jumu'ah is not a separate astronomical event: the congregation and khutbah time is set by your mosque, usually within the Dhuhr period, with further religious conditions.

Common questions

Why are my local mosque prayer times different?+

Mosques may use a different calculation convention, local adjustment or timetable. Compare the settings and confirm with the mosque.

Why does Fajr change each day?+

Earth’s position relative to the Sun changes with the seasons, moving the calculated dawn time.

Do these times work worldwide?+

Use accurate coordinates and the right timezone. High-latitude locations may need special calculation settings.

Why do two prayer applications show different Fajr times?+

They may use different dawn angles or local timetable adjustments. Check each calculation setting against the timetable followed by your local mosque.

What is the difference between prayer start and jama'ah time?+

The calculated start shows when a prayer's window opens. Jama'ah or iqamah is the congregation time set by the mosque, often later than the prayer start and not available from an astronomical calculator.

Why does the Hanafi Asr time come later?+

The Hanafi calculation commonly uses a shadow length of twice the object's length plus its noon shadow, rather than the earlier one-length standard rule. Select the school followed by your mosque.

Why can Fajr and Isha be difficult to calculate in UK summer?+

In northern areas summer twilight may persist all night. Apps use high-latitude approximation rules such as one-seventh of the night or a fraction based on the twilight angle. Verify locally.

Does sunrise mean I should start Fajr prayer?+

No. Sunrise is displayed as the end boundary for Fajr, not its start and not a separate sixth obligatory prayer. The Fajr window begins at true dawn, with its estimated opening shown in the Fajr entry.
