Aqiqah Seventh-Day Planner

Calculate a possible seventh-day Aqiqah date, compare date counting conventions, and download a reminder and practical arrangements checklist.

Aqiqah timing and arrangements

Aqiqah is an Islamic practice associated with the birth of a child. A well-known hadith mentions a sacrifice on the seventh day, along with naming and shaving the head.

How the date is counted

The planner counts the date of birth as day one for a simple seventh-day illustration. Birth near sunset and some schools’ counting conventions can change which civil date is intended, so an alternative date is also shown as a comparison.

What about the 14th or 21st day?

Some jurists discuss later opportunities if the seventh day is missed, and views vary on timing, number of animals and relevant circumstances. Do not treat this page as one universally binding opinion.

Planning the practical arrangements

Speak with a knowledgeable scholar and reputable provider about slaughter, animal welfare, food distribution, local regulations and any specific questions for your family.

01What is Aqiqah?+

Aqiqah is a sacrifice associated with welcoming a newborn. It is distinct from Eid al-Adha sacrifice and is discussed in reports about a newborn’s seventh day.

Scholarly discussions differ about its legal classification, how to handle a missed seventh day and which practices accompany it.

Sunan Abi Dawud 2838 – Seventh day↗

02How is the seventh day counted?+

A simple civil-day convention counts the Gregorian date of birth as day one, producing day seven six days later. Other approaches may exclude the birth day, and Islamic dates begin at sunset.

The calculator shows both date-counting illustrations so users can check the convention taught by their scholar; it does not determine a particular family’s religious date automatically.

Worked example:Birth on Friday 30 October: counting birth day as day one gives Thursday 5 November; beginning the count the next day produces Friday 6 November.

03Number of animals and scholarly details+

Reports in Jami at-Tirmidhi discuss two similar sheep for a boy and one for a girl. Rules on species, quality, ability, timing and substitutions require qualified guidance; users should not assume that a calculator can certify a provider or the suitability of an animal.

Use the optional note fields to plan questions for a reputable provider instead of treating these numbers as a price quotation.

Jami at-Tirmidhi 1513 – Two and one↗

04Naming, hair and feeding people+

The seventh-day report also refers to naming and shaving the child’s head, while scholars discuss related details, appropriate methods and circumstances. Be guided by safety and knowledgeable advice, particularly for infants.

Families may distribute meat or provide food in different ways. Arrange slaughter and food handling with safe, lawful, humane practices and the applicable regulations.

Sunan Abi Dawud 2838 – Naming and shaving↗

05What if the seventh day is missed?+

Some scholars discuss the 14th, 21st or later opportunities; there are differences about the supporting evidence and how those dates should be counted. The page labels them as later references rather than fixed universal deadlines.

No family should assume the religious acceptability of a plan solely from a calendar conversion.

06Practical arrangement checklist+

Confirm your understanding of the relevant ruling, decide who will carry out the sacrifice, verify reputable service provision, consider animal welfare and safe food distribution, and record contact arrangements privately.
