Dhul Hijjah Planner: first ten days, Arafah, Eid and Tashreeq

Short answer: Dhul Hijjah is the twelfth Hijri month. The first ten days are especially significant for worship, 9 Dhul Hijjah is the day of Arafah and 10 Dhul Hijjah is Eid al-Adha. The planner also marks 11–13 as the days of Tashreeq. All dates are calculated estimates until relevant official lunar calendar announcements.

Why are the first ten days of Dhul Hijjah special?

Sahih al-Bukhari 969 describes the special merit of good deeds during the first ten days of Dhul Hijjah. Practical preparations may include prayer, charity, Quran reading, remembrance and fasting on eligible days. These are not fixed daily quotas imposed by the app.

Arafah, Eid and Tashreeq dates explained

Arafah is conventionally 9 Dhul Hijjah and central to Hajj. Eid al-Adha begins on 10 Dhul Hijjah. The 11th, 12th and 13th are the days of Tashreeq. The planner shows the full first thirteen days so users do not mistake every Dhul Hijjah date for an optional fast.

Which Dhul Hijjah days may be fasted?

Voluntary fasting during appropriate early Dhul Hijjah days is encouraged in many scholarly discussions, with particular attention to Arafah for those not performing Hajj, subject to relevant views. Do not treat Eid al-Adha or ordinary Tashreeq days as ordinary voluntary fasts. Pilgrims on Arafah have different considerations.

When does Qurbani take place?

Qurbani or Udhiyah is tied to Eid al-Adha and the permitted slaughter days; juristic views differ on the final valid day and other conditions. An estimated Islamic calendar does not certify a provider's timings or a sacrifice's validity. Use the separate Qurbani Shares tool to plan finances only.

How to make a Dhul Hijjah calendar

Choose a Hijri year, review the 1–13 Dhul Hijjah rows and highlight Arafah, Eid and Tashreeq. Optionally inspect a labelled earlier/later day illustration. Export the canonical calculation as CSV or an ICS all-day planning reminder, or print the table. Recheck authoritative lunar-date announcements before sharing times or making commitments.

Worked example: the important 9–13 Dhul Hijjah sequence

Take any selected Hijri year. Day 9 is labelled Arafah, day 10 Eid al-Adha, and days 11–13 Tashreeq. The five entries use consecutive civil-date labels in the chosen calendar. The app never treats the three Tashreeq dates as ordinary White Days fasts, even where day 13 overlaps the lunar monthly 13th.

Dhul Hijjah planning for a Hajj pilgrim

Pilgrims' movements between Mina, Arafah and Muzdalifah follow detailed instructions and schedules. The day-number table is not a complete route, safety briefing or ritual checklist. Follow your authorised Hajj group, Saudi guidance and a qualified religious guide for pilgrimage timing and practices.

Why Saudi and local Dhul Hijjah dates may differ

The calendar model is Umm al-Qura, while the actual month and Eid declarations depend on the authority followed. A one-day illustrative shift cannot authenticate an announced date. The ICS file contains calculated all-day entries, not a sunset alarm or a guarantee of when Eid or Arafah will be observed.

Common questions

Are these Arafah and Eid dates officially confirmed?+

No. They are calendar estimates. Confirm the official dates with your local mosque or relevant authority.

When is the Day of Arafah?+

Arafah corresponds to 9 Dhul Hijjah. Its Gregorian date changes each year and should be confirmed locally.

Can I fast on Eid al-Adha?+

No. Eid al-Adha, 10 Dhul Hijjah, is not a day for voluntary fasting. Seek guidance for special circumstances.

Does Arafah always fall on the same weekday?+

No. The ninth of Dhul Hijjah moves through the Gregorian year, so its civil date and weekday change.
