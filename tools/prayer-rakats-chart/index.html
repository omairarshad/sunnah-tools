How many rakats in each prayer? Complete Fard and Sunnah chart

Short answer: The five daily obligatory Fard prayers consist of Fajr 2, Dhuhr 4, Asr 4, Maghrib 3 and Isha 4 rak'ahs, totalling 17 Fard rak'ahs in a normal non-travel day. Sunnah Rawatib and Witr are separate, with some details differing across Sunni schools. The chart keeps fixed Fard counts distinct from optional examples.

Fard rakats chart: Fajr, Dhuhr, Asr, Maghrib and Isha

The normal daily Fard rak'ah counts are 2 for Fajr, 4 for Dhuhr, 4 for Asr, 3 for Maghrib and 4 for Isha, for a total of 17. These are the core obligatory units. They should not be confused with voluntary or strongly recommended prayers that may be offered before or after the Fard.

Sunnah Rawatib before and after the five Fard prayers

The Prophet ﷺ encouraged regular additional prayers. Sahih Muslim 728c reports a virtue connected with twelve voluntary daily rak'ahs beyond the obligatory prayers. Commonly described Rawatib arrangements include two before Fajr, two or four before Dhuhr, two after Dhuhr, two after Maghrib and two after Isha. Scholarly schools and narrations differ in how they classify and combine particular sets. The chart labels these as examples, not another universal 12 obligatory units.

Where does Witr fit after Isha?

Witr is performed after Isha and before Fajr, but its obligatory status, numbers of rak'ahs and manner differ between schools. Hanafi teaching gives Witr a distinct wajib category, while other schools classify it as emphatically recommended. The 17-Fard total in the table does not include Witr or Tahajjud, and the app does not imply Witr is optional under every school.

What changes for Qasr on a qualifying journey?

Under recognised conditions for shortened prayer while travelling, the four-rak'ah prayers Dhuhr, Asr and Isha are shortened to two Fard rak'ahs. Fajr remains two and Maghrib remains three. Distance, duration, congregation and combining prayers are context-sensitive and differ across schools. See Quran 4:101 and our Travel Prayer Guide rather than changing the standard chart for every traveller.

Worked example: calculating 17 daily Fard rak'ahs

Add the standard non-travel Fard units: 2 (Fajr) + 4 (Dhuhr) + 4 (Asr) + 3 (Maghrib) + 4 (Isha) = 17. That is the daily standard Fard total, not a complete count of every Sunnah, Witr or night-prayer unit someone may offer.

For a qualified traveller under Qasr, the shortened sequence would be 2 + 2 + 2 + 3 + 2 = 11 Fard rak'ahs. The legal eligibility for Qasr must be checked separately.

How to read or print the rakats chart

  1. Read the first two columns for the five normal obligatory prayers and their counts.
  2. Enable Sunnah examples to see commonly discussed before-and-after patterns.
  3. Keep Witr separate and check its status under the school you follow.
  4. If travelling, review the Qasr conditions rather than automatically changing all prayers.

Why charts online disagree about total prayer rakats

Some online charts sum Fard, Sunnah Mu'akkadah, non-emphasised Sunnah, Witr and Nafl into a single total; others list only obligations. Differences can reflect narration, school and which voluntary units are counted. A number such as 17 refers to standard daily Fard only. A number including Rawatib or Witr is answering a different question, not necessarily contradicting the 17-Fard chart.

School differences and teaching children Salah

Teaching the core five Fard unit counts provides a clear starting point, but full prayer instruction involves intention, purification, posture, congregation and legal nuances. The Prophet ﷺ instructed his companions to pray as they saw him pray; see Sahih al-Bukhari 631. For practices that differ between Hanafi, Shafi'i, Maliki and Hanbali guidance, consult a qualified local teacher rather than reducing worship to a single table.
