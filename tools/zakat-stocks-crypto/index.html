Zakat on stocks, shares and cryptocurrency: full calculation guide

Short answer: Zakat on investments is not necessarily 2.5% of everything in a brokerage or crypto account. For shares acquired for resale, contemporary guidance often assesses the current full market value. Long-term shares may instead require a portion of underlying qualifying business assets. Cryptocurrencies can be currency-like, trading assets or other rights whose treatment needs careful review. See Islamic Relief UK's scholar-reviewed stocks guidance and its cryptocurrency guidance.

How to calculate Zakat on stocks, shares and ETFs

Intent and the form of ownership matter. Shares bought to trade for resale are often treated much like inventory: their current market value is assessed. For long-term investment shares, scholars may seek your proportionate ownership of the company's cash, trade stock and qualifying receivables rather than charging a flat amount on machinery, offices or other fixed assets. ETF and fund structures can require looking through multiple holdings.

The Islamic Relief UK investment guide, reviewed in February 2026 discusses both methods and an optional 25% proxy when genuine asset data is not accessible. The proxy is not a Quranic fixed percentage or mandatory global rule. You must deliberately select it only if your qualified guidance accepts it.

Dividends or cash already sitting in an exchange or brokerage account should not be counted twice if you included them under general savings. Whether a pension or a restricted investment account should be included depends on access, beneficial ownership and applicable jurisprudence.

How to assess cryptocurrency, stablecoins and utility tokens

Digital coins commonly used as a means of exchange may be assessed in a cash-like way under some contemporary approaches. Assets intentionally acquired for trade or resale can also be assessed at market value. Utility tokens, NFTs, governance rights, liquidity-pool positions, locked balances and DeFi holdings can require different analysis of the underlying rights and ownership.

Do not assume a token is either automatically Zakat-free or always fully Zakatable. Our calculator asks you to classify it and, for a utility/other token, enter a separately established qualifying percentage. See Islamic Relief UK's crypto discussion (2026). If unsure, review your asset with a scholar familiar with digital instruments rather than using a software guess.

The app does not access private keys, connect wallets, automatically convert foreign currencies or download market quotes. Values entered should be from a credible exchange or pricing source for your relevant assessment date and consistent currency.

Step-by-step stocks and crypto Zakat calculation

  1. Choose your Zakat review date and check the correct Nisab and hawl position with your school.
  2. List each share, fund or crypto holding once, with a verified current value in one currency.
  3. Mark trading/resale shares at full value only if that description matches your intention and guidance.
  4. For long-term shares, enter a supported underlying qualifying-asset percentage or deliberately choose the 25% proxy if authorised for you.
  5. For cryptocurrency, distinguish monetary/trading assets from utility rights. Get independent advice before setting the percentage of more unusual holdings.
  6. Add other qualifying assets that are not already included, deduct only permitted liabilities, and enter a verified applicable Nisab from the Nisab Calculator.
  7. Review the conditional 2.5% estimate; it is not due solely because an app produced a number.

Worked example: trading shares, long-term shares and crypto

Invented values only: Assume £3,000 of shares assessed at full trading value, £8,000 of long-term shares using an expressly accepted 25% proxy (£2,000 assessed), and £1,000 of coin holdings independently classified as fully zakatable. Add £500 in other qualifying wealth and subtract £500 in permitted debt. The assessed net wealth is £3,000 + £2,000 + £1,000 + £500 − £500 = £6,000.

With a purely hypothetical, externally selected £2,000 Nisab and a satisfied hawl, the arithmetic is £6,000 × 2.5% = £150. This £2,000 figure is not the current UK Nisab, the 25% proxy is not universal, and neither the amount nor the classifications are religiously certified.

If the underlying long-term company's zakatable asset proportion is instead 10%, that £8,000 holding contributes £800, not £2,000. The total and conditional result change accordingly. That is why classifications and source information matter more than a generic calculator formula.

Nisab, the lunar year and investment eligibility

Nisab is a gold- or silver-based minimum assessed under the school you follow. Hawl is a lunar-year condition, generally about 354–355 days, but the details of new purchases and changing holdings can vary. Our calculator will show an arithmetic-only 2.5% figure without a user-entered Nisab, but it will not label that figure payable.

For timing use the Zakat Anniversary Calculator, and for the full set of cash, gold, investments and business debts see the Zakat Calculator. The investment estimator is designed for an isolated source-backed breakdown, not for assuming that the whole contents of a trading platform are Zakatable or Sharia-compliant.

Frequently asked questions about shares and crypto Zakat

Do I pay Zakat on all stocks at 2.5%?

Not always on the full market value. Shares bought for resale are often treated as trading assets; long-term holdings may use a proportion of company zakatable assets.

Is 25% of long-term share value always zakatable?

No. A 25% proxy is one published approach for cases without available underlying asset information, not universal scholarly agreement. Select it only if accepted under your guidance.

How do I assess long-term shares directly?

Review the company or fund holdings and calculate a defensible proportion of qualifying liquid assets, stock and receivables, accounting for the relevant liabilities.

Are Bitcoin and Ethereum treated like currency?

Some contemporary scholars treat widely used cryptocurrency as cash-like or monetary. The result can depend on the token, rights, use and school.

Are NFTs and utility tokens free from Zakat?

Not automatically. Rights, intended resale and the type of underlying asset matter. Obtain a qualified percentage rather than entering zero by default.

What date should I use for asset prices?

Use the valuation date appropriate to your Zakat review and quote all amounts in a single selected currency. Historical purchase cost is usually not the current trading valuation.

Does the website connect to my stockbroker or crypto wallet?

No. You enter values manually, and the calculator does not fetch brokerage accounts, wallets or exchange prices.

What about staking, DeFi yield and token rewards?

They require separate ownership, liability and compliance analysis. Their treatment is not automatically determined by selecting a token category.

Does Nisab matter if I have only investments?

Yes. Total qualifying wealth and the applicable gold or silver benchmark remain material. Add verified Nisab using the separate calculator.

Does the calculator decide which investments are halal?

No. Sharia compliance of a company, token or income stream is a different question from mathematical Zakat assessment.

What if my investments lost value this year?

Your assessment typically involves current qualifying values, not simply purchase price or unrealised gains. Check the appropriate methodology and date.

Are pensions and retirement funds included?

Access, ownership, contribution type and fund composition can affect the ruling. Consult specialised guidance rather than using the entire headline balance automatically.
