What are the five daily Salah prayers?

Fajr, Dhuhr, Asr, Maghrib and Isha are the five daily obligatory prayers. Quran 4:103 describes prayer as appointed for believers at specified times. The tracker has five independently selectable checkboxes per recorded civil day; sunrise and optional Sunnah are not additional Fard slots. Checking one off records your own action and does not determine religious validity or acceptance.

How to use the Salah tracker

  1. Open the calculator. Today's date appears using your device's local civil date.
  2. Tick a prayer after you personally choose to record its completion.
  3. To review a previous day, choose a date within the 90-day window and adjust the boxes.
  4. Select 7, 30 or 90 days to see counts of recorded days and unrecorded days separately.
  5. Export a CSV for recorded dates, or create a JSON backup before replacing devices or clearing storage.
  6. To restore, choose a valid backup and confirm replacement; current data is not overwritten by invalid files.

Why an unrecorded date does not mean a missed Salah

Personal recordkeeping is incomplete by nature. A day with no entry means only that the user did not record checkmarks; it cannot tell if worship took place. Even a recorded day with fewer than five ticks may reflect incomplete logging. This tool does not calculate religiously missed Salah or Qada debt from these blanks. That distinction matters when planning religious obligations.

Where is my prayer history stored and who can see it?

The tracker uses the browser's local storage key for existing Salah history. It does not sync to sunnah.tools servers or require registration. Someone with access to the same browser profile or device may still view records. Clearing storage, using private browsing or changing devices can remove the history; export the JSON backup if you want to preserve it.

Worked example: recorded versus unrecorded days

In a seven-day window, suppose you recorded prayer checkmarks on four days: five ticks, four ticks, five ticks and three ticks. The summary shows four recorded days, three unrecorded days (unknown), and 17 personal prayer ticks among twenty possible slots for those recorded dates.

It would be misleading to call the remaining eighteen boxes 'missed'. The three entirely unrecorded days and any incomplete checked days may not reflect actual prayer. The tracker displays observational counts, not religious rulings.

How the CSV and JSON backup work

CSV lists dates for which an explicit record exists and marks the prayers you ticked. The JSON backup uses a validated, versioned schema compatible with the existing Salah Tracker. Restore checks dates, permitted prayer names and duplicate values and asks before replacing local history. It keeps at most the latest 90 days so your file does not grow indefinitely. There is no cloud backup or remote recovery service.

Does the calendar reset when my timezone changes?

The current day is based on the user's device-local civil date, refreshed periodically. Timezone changes or travelling can alter which civil date is currently selected; verify your travel-day entries before marking. An app date is not a substitute for local astronomical prayer starts. For prayer times use Prayer Times.

Salah tracking versus Qada, Witr and Sunnah tracking

A daily self-tracker records checkmarks. Qada concerns making up prayers determined to have been missed under a religious ruling, which can depend on why and when a prayer was omitted. Witr and recommended Sunnah prayers have school-specific details. Use a separate Qada plan only with qualified guidance. Do not mechanically import missing checkmarks as Qada obligations.

Frequently asked questions about daily salah tracker

Is this Salah tracker free?

Yes, no account or subscription is required.

Will it remind me to pray?

No. It records personal checkmarks, not phone push notifications.

Can I edit yesterday's prayer log?

Yes, choose a date within the last 90 days.

Does the app assume blank days mean missed prayers?

No. Unrecorded days remain unknown.

Are my records shared?

No server sync is used; they stay in the current browser profile.

Can I move to another phone?

Export a JSON backup and restore it into the other browser. No automatic sync exists.

Can I download CSV?

Yes. Only explicitly recorded dates are exported.

Can I track Witr?

This tracker focuses on the five Fard prayers. Witr has school-dependent rulings.

How long does it retain history?

The app retains entries from the last 90 days in local browser storage.

Does it validate my prayer?

No. A tick records a personal note, not validity, timeliness or acceptance.

How are travel days handled?

Dates follow your device's current local civil date; check records when crossing timezones.

What if my backup is invalid?

The existing history is left unchanged when the file fails schema validation.

How to use Daily Salah Tracker

Track your five daily prayers with a straightforward on-device checklist.

Tick prayers as completed throughout the day. The tracker stores data in your browser and uses the local date to start a new checklist.

Example: Record your daily prayers and inspect the history before downloading a backup.

How it works

The checklist is stored in this browser and resets for each new local day. It does not track prayer times or sync across devices.

What to check

It does not prove prayer validity, show jamaat times or sync data between phones and computers.

The record reflects only what you choose to enter.

Common questions

Can I access my checklist on another device?+

Not currently. The data is stored locally in this device’s browser.

Will my checkmarks reset tomorrow?+

Yes. The daily tracker creates a separate checklist for each local day.

Is my Salah history private?+

The checklist remains in browser storage on your device unless you share or clear it.

Can I transfer my Salah history between devices?+
