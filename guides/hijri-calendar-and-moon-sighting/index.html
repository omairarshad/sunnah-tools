How the Hijri calendar works

The Islamic (Hijri) calendar follows lunar months rather than the Gregorian solar year. It helps Muslims keep track of Ramadan, Eid, Hajj, the White Days and other important dates. A calculated date is a useful reference, but it is not necessarily the local religious announcement.

Hijri and Gregorian dates are different

A lunar year generally has 354 or 355 days, which is about 11 days shorter than a Gregorian year. The same Islamic month therefore falls at different points in the Gregorian calendar over time. An ordinary Hijri month contains 29 or 30 days.

What is the Umm al-Qura calendar?

Our converters use the Umm al-Qura calendar supported by the browser's international date-formatting functions. It provides consistent calculated dates for planning and conversion; it is not a live report of local crescent sightings. Other calculated or observational calendars can give a different result.

Why might today's Islamic date differ by one day?

Local moon-sighting decisions, the calendar system and the fact that an Islamic date begins at sunset can all affect the answer. An online conversion based on a civil date cannot identify the exact start of a religious day in every location. Confirm Ramadan, Eid and Hajj dates with your recognised local authority.

Converting and printing

Use the Hijri Date Today converter to move between Gregorian and Hijri dates. For planning, the printable Hijri calendar shows a calculated month, and the White Days tool marks the 13th, 14th and 15th with appropriate fasting caveats.

When to check locally

Do not treat a conversion as a fatwa, official new-moon decision or guaranteed local festival date. The device timezone, the moment of sunset and country-specific announcements matter. When a date is important for worship, use the guidance of your trusted community.
